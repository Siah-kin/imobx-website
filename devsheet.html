1. Homes with a path to ownership

IMOBX is preparing a rent-to-own housing project in Rio de Janeiro. A family would rent a home under an agreement explaining how it can become the owner. The organization would arrange access to property, contract construction and manage repairs and accounts.

The proposed first project is for families who have been displaced or cannot access ordinary housing finance. Location and payments need to fit each household’s circumstances, including work, school, transport and support networks.

Rafael brings construction experience and local knowledge. John works on communication and digital tools. IMOBX’s legal form, the terms for using each property and Rafael’s and John’s responsibilities still need written agreements.

How the operation would fit together

Part How it would work
Homes Documents showing ownership or the legal right to use the property, a building budget and a clear family agreement.
Money Separate records for funding, payments, costs and amounts set aside.
Decisions Defined powers for residents, staff and any investors.
Services A way to request repairs, follow progress and question decisions.

Shared designs, purchasing and administration could make further projects easier to deliver. Each family would have a route to owning its whole home. Residents would help shape shared spaces and local priorities.

The project is in preparation. This guide explains how a request would lead to completed work, a check that it meets the agreement and payment. The project reference records evidence, responsibilities and sources in Portuguese and English. The whitepaper explains the model; the roadmap sets out stages and conditions for proceeding.

2. Housing designs and building costs

Rafael’s village proposal

Rafael’s plan brings together 45 m² two-level homes around a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The illustration shows the intended community, rather than a completed development.

On 30 November 2025, Rafael supplied building calculations using CUB and SINAPI, Brazilian cost references. His revised per-home budget is:

Land 5,550
House construction, 45 square metres 18,600
Solar 1,500

Total 27,777

The plan covers at least 36 homes built together over 12 months: 36 × US$27,777 = US$999,972. These are Rafael’s dated project figures, not a finished-home price published by the indexes. A chosen site needs a budget in reais covering its actual work, permissions, services and unexpected costs. SINAPI; CUB scope.

The separate two-loft case

On 20 January 2026, Rafael reported a contract for two lofts on a steep site, awaiting its first deposit. US$27,777 covers both lofts together, equivalent to US$13,888.50 each by division. Included work, current progress and final costs still need documentation. This is a separate case from the village budget. Drawings and supplied photos.

3. Payments and home ownership

The family’s agreement

A family would rent the home and pay the agreed monthly amount in reais. Its agreement would state the purchase price and how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Its purchase record would show how much has counted towards buying the home and how much remains to pay.

For the first household, the home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay have not been set. How much of each payment would count towards buying the home also remains to be agreed. Rafael’s construction budgets are evidence for assessing building costs, not household sale offers. The household would not need to buy an investment token or use a crypto wallet. Household explanation in Portuguese and English.

Repair charges, missed payments, temporary hardship and leaving need separate terms. If a family leaves, we still need to agree what happens to the money already counted towards buying its home. No refund formula or grace period is established here. These terms belong in the offer the household assesses before agreeing to move in.

What the money would pay for

The proposed budget covers taxes, property costs, maintenance, administration, the cost of keeping promises to families and money set aside for unexpected expenses. New construction and any investor payments need their own agreed funding and terms. Donations and grants retain their specified uses.

Each amount received is counted once. An amount credited toward buying the home records the family’s progress; it is not another receipt of cash. The spending plan needs to preserve the organization’s ability to complete the promised home transfer.

When the home becomes the family’s

Once the purchase conditions are met, the required documents and registration would transfer the whole home to the household. A token transfer does not perform that property transfer. IMOBX would stop receiving rent from the home. Continued services and further construction would need their own funding; any ongoing service charge would need an actual service and terms the household agreed to.

The financial plan needs to show costs and cash through construction, occupancy and transfer. It also needs to account for delays, major repairs and periods when a household cannot pay. Funding and spending.

4. Funding construction and growth

The first project needs funding for its contracted work. Possible sources include donations, grants, service income and investment that the chosen organisation is legally allowed to offer. The token proposal also aims to fund branding and a community outreach programme. Housing, outreach and any investor payments need their own approved budgets. Token rights remain under study; the four paths range from proceeding without an IMOBX token to an investment in the operation.

New investment and later trading

Investors could buy tokens directly from IMOBX to help fund its work. We still need to agree what buyers would receive in return. A later sale between investors normally pays the seller. A separately collected trading fee could provide additional money to the project where the agreement allows it.

The housing budget needs to work through periods of little or no trading income. Accounts would show the fees actually received and their agreed use.

Money available for building

Funds committed to a trading market are unavailable for construction while that commitment lasts. The accounts would distinguish money available for bills, amounts reserved for families and repairs, and other commitments.

New investment could pay for agreed work before families start paying rent. First, IMOBX needs its legal form, documents showing the right to use the property, a budget and a legally allowed way to raise that money. Forecast rent is not money already received. Payments to investors depend on the chosen agreement and actual available funds. Funding questions.

How outreach work would be paid

An approved campaign budget would pay for specific tasks, with the work and payment terms written down first. A participant would submit their work; a reviewer would check that it matches the agreed task before approving payment. The community tool could show submission, review and payment status. Paid participants need to disclose compensation and follow each channel’s rules.

Work payments and passive investment income require separate terms. The token model and its rights remain under research. Residents would not need to promote the project to retain housing or decision rights.

5. Participation and decision rights

A family’s housing agreement, its membership voice and an investment are separate relationships. One person could have more than one role.

Participant What needs to be clear
Families Affordable payments, repairs, purchase progress, hardship, leaving and whole-home transfer.
Property contributors Donation, lease or sale terms; title, debts and transfer costs.
Staff and resident representatives Powers, pay, appointment, spending limits, reporting and replacement.
Builders and suppliers Work, price, payment stages and independent completion checks.
Donors and social funders Permitted use, reports, delays and cancellation.
Possible investors Who would create the token, what buyers could receive, where income would come from, costs, possible losses, voting, sales and leaving.
Neighbours Local impacts, services, access and a way to raise concerns.
Independent checkers Authority and access to review property, safety, accounts and agreements.

Study-room hours, courtyard use, shared-space repair priorities and representative selection are examples of resident decisions. One option under discussion is one vote per member for those matters. Staff would have agreed duties and spending limits, with someone else checking completed work. Investor rights would follow the selected investment agreement.

A household’s purchase terms would follow its agreement. Buying tokens would not give control over another family’s contract. The voting procedure, spending limits and dispute review process remain to be developed with participants. Decisions and repair examples.

Ostrom’s work informs questions about membership, shared rules, checking work and resolving disputes. These questions apply to facilities such as solar equipment, the orchard and study rooms. Decision examples; participant map; CVM guidance.

6. Records, payments and support

The proposed digital services would help people read agreements, request repairs, follow work and check approved information. Private family documents would stay outside the public website and blockchain.

Record or service Purpose
Project website Explain the proposal, current progress, costs and documents.
Support channel Receive questions and requests, with a visible response and status.
Legal records Document the legal right to use the property, family agreements and any investment.
Accounts Connect each amount received and paid to its purpose and evidence.
Shared work record Show responsibilities, decisions, deadlines and work checked against the agreement.

Following a payment

Families would pay in reais. IMOBX would keep money for Brazilian bills. Any approved digital payment needs a provider that accepts the organization and its activities, a record of conversion costs and a tested return to the bank. No provider is confirmed for IMOBX. Proposed money path.

If an investment permits income payments, software could maintain a record of what each eligible person may claim. Running costs would include setup, individual claims, corrections and support. No operating-cost saving is established yet.

How a repair would move forward

A resident reports a leak. The maintenance role assesses it and obtains a quote. Spending follows the agreed budget and approval rule. Another person checks the completed repair; the accounts link its payment and receipt. The resident can question the result. Records of unfinished work support a replacement when someone leaves a role.

The complete process, including payment failures and lost access, still needs to be demonstrated. A public report would show the approved budget, the work completed, who checked it and the amount paid. It would identify who prepared and reviewed the accounts. Private identity and household documents would remain outside the public site. Everyday decisions and tasks.

7. Development stages

IMOBX’s development plan connects the first homes in Rio with resident participation, transparent accounts and additional funding. Legal preparation, community discussions and digital trials can progress in parallel. Construction dates depend on each project, funding and permissions.

Stage Intended result
Legal structure and property A responsible organization, documents showing the legal right to use the property and reviewed housing agreements.
Resident participation A say in shared matters without buying investment tokens, with defined budgets and review procedures.
First homes Funded construction, safe occupancy and records of household payments and purchase progress.
Payment records people can check A demonstration of USDC received and payments approved on Arc, linked to accounts and evidence.
Funding and account review Records of where funding would come from, which spending is approved and how promises to families would be paid for.
More homes and owner partnerships Further projects with local teams and possible rent-to-own services for participating owners.

Following an operating project

The proposed monthly report would show receipts and payments, amounts set aside, building costs and delays, repairs, unresolved issues and families’ purchase progress. Every amount received and paid would be checked against its records. Affordability and service-response targets need to be agreed locally.

A first expansion review is proposed after at least three operating months. This is a review point, not proof of long-term viability. A second local team should be able to repeat the process, with funding for new work that respects existing commitments. The detailed roadmap explains who would do the work, what must be ready and what would stop a stage. Named appointments and delivery dates remain open.

What the public can follow

The website explains the proposal now. As the project develops, reports would show signed agreements, approved work, funding received, construction and home transfers. Any investment access would follow defined rights, legal review and checked payment arrangements. Detailed roadmap.
