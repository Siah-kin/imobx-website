# The IMOBX token: how it works and what it is for

Draft, 9 November 2026. This paper describes a planned design. The token has not been issued, and the design may change before launch.

## What the token is for

IMOBX is preparing a rent-to-own housing project in Rio de Janeiro, organised as a neighbourhood cooperative. The token is a way for the community that grows around the houses to earn an income of its own, so that its work runs on what it has earned and not on promises.

We call it a self-compounding community-funding token. Every time it changes hands, a fee is paid. The fee pays for the community's work, for new houses and for the token's own pool, so the project grows from what it earns.

The token is optional. Families who rent to own with IMOBX join the housing cooperative as members, and membership is all they need. No token is ever a condition for a family's housing. The token comes at a later stage of the project.

## What the token is

The IMOBX token is a crypto token. People can buy and sell it, and its price moves. It is a volatile asset, and this paper says so plainly.

IMOBX builds on the self-compounding model of Ethervista. Its rules:

- There are 1,000,000 tokens, once and for all. None are created later.
- There is no presale. Nobody buys tokens from IMOBX before trading opens, and there is no early round for investors.
- Three quarters of the tokens go into the trading pool, locked there so nobody can take them out. The other quarter is set aside: 5% for partners, 5% for operations, 5% for community rewards, 5% for governance, 3% for brokers and 2% for legal costs.
- The team receives no tokens.

Most token projects raise funds by selling tokens to early investors or by creating new ones as rewards. IMOBX does neither. Its income is the fee on each trade.

## Where each trade's fee goes

Each trade pays a fee. The token would first trade on Arctide, where the fee is 14% of the amount. Arctide adds its own 0.25%. A second exchange, Argus, charges 11% and keeps 30% of it.

On Arctide, the 14% divides this way:

- 45% pays wages for the people hired to do the community's work.
- 35% goes to the pool. The pool is the shared reserve through which the token changes hands.
- 20% goes to people who lock their tokens.

For a trade of US$100, that is US$6.30 for wages, US$4.90 for the pool and US$2.80 for people who lock their tokens, out of a US$14 fee.

When IMOBX puts in the pool's starting funds, it holds three quarters of the pool, and its part of the pool's share goes to new houses. The rest goes to people who add their own funds to the pool.

Before any benefit for people who lock their tokens applies, we will name it and publish its terms.

## How the token would open

The token can open in two ways, and both stay possible.

**A seeded pool.** IMOBX puts the pool's first funds in itself. The deeper the pool is, the more it can handle when trading opens.

**Arctide's bonding curve.** This is a contract that sells tokens at a rising price until a sale goal is reached. At launch, almost all of what buyers paid goes into the trading pool, and a small part goes to whoever triggers the launch. The first buyers' funds start the pool, so the pool's share of the fee goes to Arctide. New houses get the share earned by funds that IMOBX adds itself.

On either route, the first buyers' payments go into the pool, and the project's income stays the fee.

## How the first house is funded

The first house is funded separately from the token. Once the cooperative is registered, it plans to run a donation or reward campaign in reais on a Brazilian platform, and to apply for grants from foundations. People who back that campaign never receive tokens for it, and the campaign runs before the token launches.

The token's fee adds to the budget for new houses over time. It does not pay for the first house. Fees tend to arrive in a burst soon after a launch. On Arctide, weekly fees fell from US$9,845 in the first week to under US$200 by late October 2026. The token is not a steady source of income, so nobody should plan a house around it.

## The four V's

The token's purpose is set by four rules, and they apply to all of the community's funding.

**Volume.** The community's work runs on income it has earned, spent under a budget its members agree. Each family's payment first covers what the family has committed to: building, maintenance and a possible exit payment. Wages, new houses and reserves come from what is left. The token's fee adds to that.

**Verify.** Anyone can check where the fees went. Every payment the project makes is open too, with the name of who approved it. Personal details stay private. A record shows that a payment was made. Independent checks and the supporting documents show whether the work was done.

**Vote.** Housing decisions stay one member, one vote. Holding more tokens adds nothing to anyone's vote on housing, and neither do more posts or more time online. On the token's own rules, the weight of a vote comes mainly from work that other people have confirmed.

**Vow.** Some commitments no vote can break, starting with each family's housing agreement. A vote cannot change a family's housing agreement. Only the family and IMOBX together can, under Brazilian law.

## What the token does not do

The token gives no share in IMOBX and no claim on any house, plot of land or family payment. The fee figures in this paper describe the design and are not payments anyone is promised.

Families do not need the token to use anything the community offers as members. Services run on membership, and the token is not an entry ticket.

## Risks

- **The price can fall a long way.** It moves with every trade. A small pool can make the token hard to sell.
- **Trading usually shrinks after a launch.** The fee income can fall as fast as it arrived.
- **The network and its currency are run by companies.** Arc is a network and USDC is a digital dollar, and companies operate both. The company that issues USDC can freeze it.
- **The rules for crypto-assets are changing** in Brazil, Europe and the United States. A new rule can force changes to the token.
- **A software fault or a lost wallet key** can mean losing tokens for good. A crypto-asset has no deposit insurance, and Brazil's guarantee fund (FGC) does not cover it.
