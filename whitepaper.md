# IMOBX

## A rent-to-own housing proposal for Rio

**Project paper · Version 0.8, 24 October 2026**

IMOBX is preparing a rent-to-own housing project in Rio de Janeiro. A family rents a home, part of each payment counts towards buying it, and the whole home is transferred to the family once the agreed conditions are met. The plan brings together local building work, written agreements, maintenance and accounts people can check.

**What exists today.** Rafael’s November 2025 village budget; his January 2026 report on a two-loft contract; drawings; a village illustration made with an image generator; and construction photographs supplied by the team. The drawings and photographs are not a listing of available houses.

**What is not decided.** The property. The home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay, and how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Repairs, missed payments and leaving. IMOBX’s legal form, and Rafael’s and John’s responsibilities, which need written agreements. Funding and what funders receive. The rights of any token, which is a digital record of a right that can be bought and sold.

Rafael describes his construction experience and knowledge of local conditions; his references are not yet listed. John works on community communication and digital tools.

Under the plan, a family rents the home and makes monthly payments in reais. The agreement records the amount credited towards the purchase and the remaining agreed price. Ownership of the whole home passes to the household once the purchase conditions and property transfer are completed. IMOBX then stops receiving rent from that house.

The [household explanation](rent-to-own.html) covers payments, repairs, decisions and leaving in Portuguese and English. The [dated project reference](reference.html) identifies sources and current responsibilities; its [chapter map](reference.html#chapters) connects the available material to Rafael’s longer manuscript.

## 1. Who the pilot is for

The first project is for families who have been displaced or cannot use ordinary housing finance. Who can join, what they can afford and which locations meet their needs are still to be agreed with local participants. A move has to make sense for the family’s work, school, transport and support network.

Housing problems exist worldwide; [UN-Habitat’s page on adequate housing](https://unhabitat.org/11-1-adequate-housing) gives general background and does not describe any IMOBX family. In Brazil, favelas and urban communities have their own histories, businesses and relationships. [IBGE’s terminology page](https://www.ibge.gov.br/geociencias/organizacao-do-territorio/tipologias-do-territorio/15788-favelas-ecomunidades-urbanas.html?edicao=38944&t=o-que-e) explains the term “favelas and urban communities”. The [2022 Census release](https://agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br/agencia-noticias/2012-agencia-de-noticias/noticias/41797-censo-2022-brasil-tinha-16-4-milhoes-de-pessoas-morando-em-favelas-e-comunidades-urbanas) counted around 16.4 million residents in these areas, 8.1% of the population. That national figure does not tell us the condition of a particular home or family. Living in a favela does not itself mean someone is displaced.

### How a household moves from renting to owning

A family takes three steps towards owning the home. First, IMOBX and the household agree the home, purchase price, monthly payment and amount credited towards purchase. Then each payment updates the household’s purchase record. Finally, once the purchase conditions are met, the required documents and registration transfer the whole home to the household.

The purchase record shows the total paid and the part of that funding that counts towards buying the home. Only that agreed part reduces the price still owed. It is part of the same payment, not extra income for IMOBX. Neither the village construction budget nor the separate two-loft contract is an announced sale price for a household.

Repair charges, late payments, temporary hardship and leaving the arrangement need their own agreed terms, and none is set. No refund formula or grace period has been selected. Before moving in, a household needs to know how the amounts already counted towards buying the home are treated if it leaves or if IMOBX cannot continue. These are open terms of the first offer, not decisions already made on the household’s behalf. See [payments and transfer](rent-to-own.html#payments) and [hardship and leaving](rent-to-own.html#hardship).

## 2. The housing and delivery arrangement

Under the plan, IMOBX owns each included property or holds a written legal right to operate it. It signs agreements with families and hires builders and other providers. Each property needs a file showing its owner, debts or restrictions, permissions, condition, building plan, budget and responsible people.

A property can reach IMOBX by donation, lease, sale or another agreement, and the property’s agreement says which. A letter saying someone intends to donate is different from a completed transfer. Ownership needs the required property registration.

One structure under consideration is a foreign organization holding Brazilian property. [Receita Federal’s guidance for legal entities based abroad](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/orientacao-tributaria/cadastros/cnpj/solicitacao-de-atos-perante-o-cnpj-por-meio-da-internet/orientacoes-para-pessoa-juridica-domiciliada-no-exterior) confirms that such an organization needs a CNPJ, Brazil’s business tax registration. A CNPJ does not give property ownership or make a bank or exchange accept the organization; these are separate matters. IMOBX has not chosen its legal form and no property is confirmed here, so both still need documents.

Rafael’s building company is a contractor under consideration. Its catalogue can show experience once the completed work, and permission to publish it, have been checked. Under the plan, a founder who is connected to a builder does not approve that builder’s bill. Someone else checks the price, profit, changes and completed work.

### Rafael's village vision, 30 November 2025

Rafael’s vision is 45 m² two-level houses facing a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The aim is a path to ownership, lower household costs and places to meet and learn. The [illustration](properties.html#visao-rafael) shows this vision, not a completed village, and it was made with an image generator.

Under the plan, residents agree who cares for shared spaces, how study rooms are used and how repairs are paid for. Solar savings depend on real equipment and usage figures, and some electricity charges remain. [ANEEL’s distributed-generation guidance](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/assuntos/geracao-distribuida/) sets out the rules for small solar systems; it gives no figures for these houses.

## 3. The cost proposal already supplied

Rafael prepared a **US$27,777 budget per 45 m² house** using CUB and SINAPI, Brazilian building-cost references, and his purchasing and construction plan. On 30 November 2025, he showed a US$21,883.86 construction calculation plus US$5,550 for land. He then supplied this revised budget:

| Item | Rafael’s budget, USD per house |
|---|---:|
| Land | 5,550 |
| House construction, 45 square metres | 18,600 |
| Solar | 1,500 |
| Shared infrastructure and facilities | 1,500 |
| Legal and design | 627 |
| **Total** | **27,777** |

His plan calls for at least 36 houses built together: **US$999,972 over 12 months**. Buying directly and sharing building work and facilities are part of the savings plan. Each actual build must record its price date, quantities, included work, costs in reais and invoices.

These are Rafael’s project figures, not a finished-house price published by a cost index. [SINAPI](https://www.caixa.gov.br/poder-publico/modernizacao-gestao/sinapi/paginas/default.aspx) is the Caixa reference for construction costs and inputs; a specific budget still needs its own location, date and scope. CUB leaves out some site works, connections, design and builder payments, as the [CUB scope page](https://sinduscon-caxias.net.br/servicos/cub) explains. A saving has to be explained through the work and purchasing plan; deducting a presumed profit percentage does not prove it.

### The separate two-loft case

The included work, current progress and final costs are not yet documented. This is separate from the village budget. See the [drawings and photos](properties.html#estudo-lofts).

For the first project, each family’s agreement needs affordable payments in reais and clear rules for price changes. The payment amount and purchase term are open, as the status at the top of this paper says.

## 4. How the funding would work

The financial plan covers houses and continued service. The rules for payment and reinvestment are not agreed.

The accounts identify each payer, purpose and permitted use, with separate records for rent, donations, grants, investments, service income and trading fees. If someone buys a token from another holder, the sale funding normally goes to the seller. Only a separately collected project fee goes to IMOBX.

Under the plan, funding is used in this order:

1. Record the funding actually received.
2. Set aside taxes and amounts promised for a specific purpose.
3. Pay agreed property, maintenance and administration bills.
4. Keep funding for repairs, empty houses, promises made to families and unexpected costs.
5. Fund new houses under the agreed budget.
6. Pay investors only if funding remains and a valid agreement allows it.

Payments credited toward a family’s purchase are a commitment under its agreement. They are not a second receipt of cash. Using the funding elsewhere must not make the promised home transfer impossible. Funding locked in a trading market also cannot pay a building bill at the same time.

## 5. Trade fees as supplementary funding

The first houses are funded through the routes in section 4: founders, partners, grants and donations. Building never waits on token trading: we start building a house only when its funding is secured.

Once the token can be used, each trade on the market pays a small fee in USDC. A published rule states which fees IMOBX receives, what they pay for and how receipts are checked; before launch, we publish the fee rate and who can change it. A sale between two holders pays the seller; it does not provide a building budget.

The fees pay for work that has been checked, such as outreach tasks set out in writing, each payment its own line in the accounts. They depend on trading, so the plan is tested with little or no trading income. Higher fees may discourage buyers and sellers.

A token’s market price does not establish funding available to pay building bills. Eligibility, review and disclosure of payment are defined before any outreach programme pays participants.

## 6. What self-sufficiency means

Self-sufficiency has two separate tests. The first is whether recurring income covers IMOBX’s regular bills, repairs and the cost of keeping promises to families, which keeps the existing operation going. The second is building more houses, which needs additional funding.

When a family becomes the owner, IMOBX stops receiving rent from that house. Forecasts must show how income continues afterward. Any ongoing service charge needs a real service, an affordable price and terms the resident agreed to.

A one-off grant can help build, but it is not continuing income. Having 1,000 houses does not by itself show that the accounts balance. We need to follow groups of houses from building through payments to ownership.

## 7. Resident voice and accountable governance

We are designing how decisions are shared: who decides what, and how software helps everyone follow the agreed rules. Residents, property contributors, builders, staff, donors, investors, neighbours and independent checkers need clear roles. We have mapped their likely needs for discussion, not confirmed them through interviews.

Elinor Ostrom studied how people manage shared resources, and her [Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/) is the research basis for the principles we use. Applying them to IMOBX is the project’s own step; the lecture does not endorse IMOBX. We apply these lessons: define the group, involve affected people, check work and spending, resolve disputes and keep local decisions close to residents.

| Who or what | Rule under discussion |
|---|---|
| Families | Affordable payments, repairs and a written path to owning the whole home. |
| Property contributors | Clear donation, sale or lease terms and responsibility for debts and transfer costs. |
| Builders and suppliers | Agreed work, price, payment dates and independent checks of the completed work. |
| Staff and resident representatives | Clear powers, spending limits, appointment, reporting and replacement. |
| Donors and social funders | Agreed use of funds and evidence of housing results. |
| Possible investors | Written rights stating what investors would receive, costs, risk of loss, decision powers and how they sell or leave. |
| Neighbours | A voice on local impacts, services and access. |
| Independent checkers | Access and authority to review property, safety, accounts and agreements. |

Examples of resident decisions are study-room hours, courtyard use and the order of shared-space repairs. Cooperative decisions follow one member, one vote, as Brazil’s cooperative law sets out (Lei 5.764, article 42), with representatives who can be replaced. The [International Cooperative Alliance’s statement of cooperative identity](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity) describes democratic member control; it does not mean IMOBX is a registered cooperative. People handling maintenance act within an agreed budget, and someone else checks the work and bill. A resident who disagrees with a charge can ask for it to be checked.

Whatever a vote decides, including a token vote, only the family and IMOBX together, under the law, can change a family’s housing agreement. Holding tokens gives no say over another household’s purchase agreement. Residents take part as members, whether or not they hold tokens or do promotional work. The voting procedure, spending limits, repair responsibilities and review process are not designed yet; they are developed with participants. See [resident decisions and repairs](rent-to-own.html#decisions) and [who decides, and when](governance.html).

## 8. What the token is for

The token is for using the community’s services and goods, and it launches only once it gives that access. Until then, the work is funded through the routes in section 4. A Brazilian lawyer is reviewing the design; what holders receive is set after that review, under the rules of Brazil’s securities-market regulator, the CVM.

It is designed as a loop. People use the token for the community’s services and goods. Each trade on the market pays a small fee in USDC. The fees pay for checked work, and that work adds services members and token holders can use. The token’s price is left to the market.

Alongside the loop, the token has three functions:

| Function | What it means |
|---|---|
| Volume | Trading fees, paid in USDC, pay for checked work. Before launch, we publish the fee rate and who can change it. |
| Voting | Holders vote on the token’s own rules. Cooperative matters stay one member, one vote. Before launch, we publish who controls the token’s admin keys, meaning who can change its settings. |
| Verify | In the plan, anyone can check each payment and who approved it, with or without the token. |

Locking tokens is a pledge to stay with the project for a set period; the tokens return at the end, and a longer lock gives the same vote. What a lock gives a holder is set after the lawyer’s review.

Once the token can be used, it launches on a public market on the Arc blockchain, open to anyone from the first day.

The outreach programme sets out tasks in writing, checks the work submitted and pays for work that meets the agreement from funding already set aside. A community tool can show the task, who checked the work and whether it was paid for. Housing rights and residents’ voice stay separate from this programme.

The plan is that each family owns its **whole house**. In Brazil, the property registry records who owns a house (Civil Code, article 1,245); the token carries no claim on any house.

The nonprofit idea is meant to keep funding serving housing, and IMOBX’s legal form is not decided. [Law 13.019](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l13019compilado.htm), which covers partnerships with the public sector, defines nonprofits as organisations that use their results for their social purpose (article 2(I)(a)). Brazilian advisers review any funding arrangement the chosen legal form permits. A donation given for building houses keeps its agreed purpose.

The CVM looks at the rights a token actually gives, not at names such as “utility” or “governance”, in its [Guidance Opinion 40](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/legislacao/pareceres-orientacao/pare040.html). That opinion explains how the CVM assesses tokens; it does not approve any IMOBX offer. This paper claims no regulatory approval.

Our commitments, which partners and families can hold us to:

- We start building a house only when its funding is secured; trading supports the work around it.
- We show progress with proof and leave the price to the market.
- The cooperative’s members agree the token’s rules before it launches.

Promoters disclose any payment they receive and follow the rules of each channel and country they reach. See [the token on the website](blockchain.html#token).

## 9. What becomes digital

Under the plan, people can follow funding in and out, see agreed decisions and check payments. A blockchain is a shared digital record that shows who approved a payment and when, and it can hold the payment record. It does not check the quality of a roof or replace the family’s agreement.

Public markets, or trading platforms, already run on the Arc network, a blockchain.

For the first project, we compare a shared digital record with ordinary accounts and signed agreements: what gets easier to check, how much it costs, who can correct an error and who keeps access. A token cannot create rental income, verify a building or guarantee a buyer when someone wants to leave. The [OECD’s study of tokenising assets in financial markets](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/11/understanding-the-tokenisation-of-assets-in-financial-markets_2e657111/c033401a-en.pdf) is general background on tokenisation; it does not review IMOBX.

Residents and the organization agree who decides each matter, spending limits and how a decision can be reviewed. Everyday work follows these rules without waiting for instructions from the founder.

For example, a resident reports a leak through the support channel. The person handling maintenance assesses it, gets a quote and follows the agreed spending rule. Someone else checks the repair. The payment and receipt go into the accounts. Residents can raise a problem and ask for review.

Tasks need a responsible person, deadline and budget. If someone leaves a role, their replacement finds the open work, approvals and documents needed to continue. Residents need ways to choose and replace responsible people. Personal information stays private.

Software assistants can organize documents and remind people about unfinished work, and blockchain records can show payment amounts and destinations. The agreed rules still determine who decides or spends. This method has not been tested: it needs agreement with participants and a working pilot. See [daily decisions and tasks](transparency.html#faq-flock-siah).

Families pay in reais. IMOBX keeps funding for Brazilian bills and uses digital payments only for an agreed purpose. Any conversion needs an accepted provider, a full cost record and a tested route back to the bank. No provider is confirmed for IMOBX. Residents do not need crypto wallets. See the [funding path](transparency.html#settlement) and the [payment-service requirements](transparency.html#ramp-options).

## 10. Pilot roadmap and evidence

The [roadmap](roadmap.html#stages) follows six stages: legal structure and property, resident participation, first houses, payment records people can check, funding and account review, and further projects. Each stage explains who does the work, what must happen first and which records are needed before it proceeds. Construction dates depend on the chosen project, funding and permissions. Legal preparation, resident discussions and digital trials can progress alongside one another.

After families move in, the plan follows at least three months before reviewing expansion. A second local team should be able to repeat the work. The website can report real progress and accounts before any token exists. A later purchase screen follows agreed rights and a checked legal and payment route.

| What to follow | What to show |
|---|---|
| Houses and agreements | Written proof of the right to use the property and a signed family agreement before moving in. |
| Building | What was delivered, actual cost and any delay. |
| Funding | Monthly checks of all funding received, paid and set aside. |
| Families | Repair requests, unresolved issues, participation and purchase progress. |
| Continuing the project | Bills covered during low trading and funding for the next houses. |

Service targets depend on the first project’s needs and resident participation. An unresolved property right, funding gap or unsafe building prevents the affected stage from proceeding. Expansion requires evidence that there is enough funding for promises already made. Token prices and holder counts do not measure how well a family is housed.

The [participation page](contact.html) has separate routes for residents, property owners, building professionals, community organisations, funding partners and community contributors. A household can describe its location and housing needs. An owner can describe a property and whether donation, sale or lease is being considered. A builder can introduce a service and area of work. A funding partner can state the type of support and documents it needs to assess.

Receiving contacts are not yet available. Visitors can prepare and copy an introduction, but the site does not send it or reserve housing, appoint a contractor or accept an investment. Routes: [household](contact.html?role=resident), [property](contact.html?role=property) and [funding](contact.html?role=funding).

The partnership under discussion combines IMOBX as project organiser, OLYMPUS as a possible builder, linked to Rafael, and a community or charitable organisation as a possible property or local partner. A financing partner can support an agreed project budget. These relationships are proposals, with no partner agreement or funding commitment established here. A property visit can produce a condition assessment and a costed next step.

Builders, legal advisers and accountants review their own areas. Families help shape the service before rules are fixed. If a builder is connected to an IMOBX founder, other people must compare prices, approve the contract and inspect the completed work. See [people and appointment status](reference.html#people).

## 12. Risks and what still needs checking

Rafael supplied the November budget and January two-loft account. Their dates and different purposes matter. Designs show intended buildings, not completed houses. No number of donated properties or received funding is confirmed here.

The legal registration, property documents, permissions and signed agreements need checking for the chosen project. The researched payment services do not establish a working IMOBX connection. Resident voting, reinvestment and token rights are decisions still to be agreed.

The public [risk list](reference.html#risks) identifies construction, household, cash, offering and software risks, with reviewing roles still to be appointed. The complete financial model is not ready yet: it needs site costs in reais, affordable payments, repair and delay scenarios, and zero token-trading income. This paper does not show that income covers all costs or that the project can work across Brazil.

The CVM’s [Resolution 249](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/200/resol249.htm) of 13 October 2026 creates an experimental programme for distributed-ledger technology, the technical name for shared digital records such as a blockchain. Article 1, paragraph 3 excludes real investors, actually issued securities and real-money movements in those tests; it does not authorise a public offering. The project’s legal form and any way to raise the funding need their own review.

Project updates show agreements signed, funding received and work completed, alongside open questions and responsible roles. The [source list](reference.html#sources) shows which statement each source supports. The [version record](reference.html#versions) identifies this edition for citation; private household documents stay unpublished.
