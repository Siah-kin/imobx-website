# IMOBX

## A documented rent-to-own housing model for Rio

**Project paper v0.2 · 15 October 2026 · draft for founders and partners**

IMOBX wants to help families rent a home and follow a clear path to buying it. We are preparing a first housing project in Rio de Janeiro. The plan brings together local building work, written agreements, maintenance and accounts people can check.

Rafael brings construction experience and knowledge of local conditions. John works on community communication and digital tools. Their responsibilities and IMOBX’s legal form still need to be agreed in writing.

We want each family to understand what it pays, what counts toward buying the home and who handles repairs. Supporters should be able to see where their money goes. IMOBX needs enough money to meet these commitments before starting more homes.

This paper explains the plan and the decisions still needed. It does not establish donated properties, a token sale or a right to investment income.

## 1. Who the pilot is for

The first project would serve families who have been displaced or cannot use ordinary housing finance. With local participants, we need to agree who can join, what they can afford and which locations meet their needs. A move must make sense for the family’s work, school, transport and support network.

Housing problems exist worldwide. In Brazil, favelas and urban communities have their own histories, businesses and relationships. Brazil’s 2022 Census counted around 16.4 million residents in these areas, 8.1% of the population. That national figure does not tell us the condition of a particular home or family. Living in a favela does not itself mean someone is displaced. [IBGE terminology](https://www.ibge.gov.br/geociencias/organizacao-do-territorio/tipologias-do-territorio/15788-favelas-ecomunidades-urbanas.html?edicao=38944&t=o-que-e); [2022 Census](https://agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br/agencia-noticias/2012-agencia-de-noticias/noticias/41797-censo-2022-brasil-tinha-16-4-milhoes-de-pessoas-morando-em-favelas-e-comunidades-urbanas); [UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/11-1-adequate-housing).

Before anyone joins, the agreement must explain payments, the purchase price, repairs, late payments, hardship, leaving the program and what happens if IMOBX cannot continue. Families need a practical way to get help and challenge a decision.

## 2. The housing and delivery arrangement

IMOBX would own each included property or have a written legal right to operate it. It would sign agreements with families and hire builders and other providers. Each property needs a file showing its owner, debts or restrictions, permissions, condition, building plan, budget and responsible people.

A property might be donated, leased, sold or made available under another agreement. Record which one applies. A letter saying someone intends to donate is different from a completed transfer. Ownership needs the required property registration.

July’s discussions considered a foreign organization holding Brazilian property. Receita Federal confirms that such an organization needs a CNPJ, Brazil’s business tax registration. Registration, property ownership and acceptance by a bank or exchange are separate matters. IMOBX’s chosen legal form and any received properties still need documents. [Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/orientacao-tributaria/cadastros/cnpj/solicitacao-de-atos-perante-o-cnpj-por-meio-da-internet/orientacoes-para-pessoa-juridica-domiciliada-no-exterior).

Rafael’s building company is a possible contractor. Its catalogue can show experience when completed work and permission to publish it are checked. A founder linked to a builder should not approve that builder’s own bill: price, profit, changes and delivery need checks by someone else.

### Rafael's village vision, 30 November 2025

Rafael proposed 45 m² two-level homes facing a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The aim is a path to ownership, lower household costs and places to meet and learn. The [illustration](properties.html#visao-rafael) shows this vision, not a completed village.

Residents would help agree who cares for shared spaces, how study rooms are used and how repairs are paid for. Solar savings need real equipment and usage figures; some electricity charges remain. [ANEEL guidance](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/assuntos/geracao-distribuida/).

## 4. How the money would work

The current plan puts homes and continued service first. Earlier percentage splits are not agreed rules.

Record who paid, why they paid and what the money may be used for. Keep rent, donations, grants, investments, service income and trading fees separately identified. If someone buys a token from another holder, the sale money normally goes to the seller. Only a separately collected project fee goes to IMOBX.

The proposed order is:

1. Record the money actually received.
2. Set aside taxes and amounts promised for a specific purpose.
3. Pay agreed property, maintenance and administration bills.
4. Keep money for repairs, empty homes, resident commitments and unexpected costs.
5. Fund new homes under the agreed budget.
6. Consider investor payments only if money remains and a valid agreement allows them.

Payments credited toward a family’s purchase are a commitment under its agreement. They are not a second receipt of cash. Using the money elsewhere must not make the promised home transfer impossible. Money locked in a trading market also cannot pay a building bill at the same time.

## 5. Trade fees as supplementary funding

Arctide is an app for creating and trading tokens. Its markets charge fees in USDC, a digital currency linked to the US dollar. Fees are split between people funding trading, the project and the platform. The early sale stage, called a bonding curve, has no trading fee. IMOBX would need to show which later fees it has a right to receive and that they reached its account. [Arctide documentation](https://github.com/0xRustyDev/arctide-docs/blob/main/README.md).

These receipts could help fund agreed work without creating more IMOBX tokens for that payment. They still depend on trading. Test the plan with little or no trading income; higher fees may discourage buyers and sellers.

The proposed US$100,000 starting fund is an example, not committed money. A token’s market value does not mean that amount is available to build homes.

## 6. What self-sufficiency means

IMOBX would support its existing operation when recurring income covers its regular bills, repairs and commitments to families. Building more homes needs additional money. These are two separate tests.

When a family becomes the owner, IMOBX stops receiving rent from that home. Forecasts must show how income continues afterward. Any ongoing service charge needs a real service, an affordable price and terms the resident agreed to.

A one-off grant can help build, but it is not continuing income. Having 1,000 homes does not by itself show that the accounts balance. We need to follow groups of homes from building through payments to ownership. Reviews after three or six years are possible checkpoints, not promised payout dates.

## 7. Resident voice and accountable governance

We are developing a DAO: an organization where people share decisions and use software to help follow the agreed rules. Residents, future residents, builders, staff and funders need to help define their roles.

Ostrom studied how people manage shared resources. We want to apply useful lessons: define the group, involve affected people, check work and spending, resolve disputes and keep local decisions close to residents. [Ostrom’s lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/).

| Who or what | Proposed rule to discuss |
|---|---|
| A family’s home and purchase | Follow its agreement and law; a token vote cannot rewrite it on its own. |
| Shared spaces and local repairs | Residents help decide within the budget and safety rules. |
| Building and payments | Named people approve, check and pay, with separate checks. |
| New projects | Agreed bodies decide, with local representation and reports. |
| Investors | Receive only the rights in their written agreement. |

One option is equal member voice in resident matters and elected people responsible for work. Experience can help choose someone for a time-limited role; online activity need not become votes. Newcomers need a way to participate. Resting, illness or leaving does not erase recognized work. Housing rights do not depend on a score. No resident token plan is agreed. [Cooperative principles](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity); [decision examples](roadmap.html#governance).

## 8. What a token could represent

We are studying whether a token could give people a written right to part of the money genuinely left over after costs, commitments and savings for future needs. We have not chosen its rights, income source or legal arrangement. No current payment right is established.

We want each family to be able to own its **whole home**. Sharing purchasing and administration across projects could save work. The proposal does not split every house between token buyers. A future investment linked to IMOBX or several projects would need to state which properties, debts, income and shared risks it includes.

The nonprofit idea is intended to keep money serving housing. Its legal form remains undecided. Law 13.019, covering public-sector partnerships, defines nonprofits that use their results for their social purpose. We cannot simply promise those results as investor dividends. Brazilian advisers need to review participation without profit rights and any other permitted investment arrangement. A donation given for building homes must keep its agreed purpose. [Law 13.019, article 2(I)(a)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l13019compilado.htm).

A membership record and an investment do different jobs. A membership record does not itself give property ownership or rental income. If an investment is offered, its agreement must say who owes what, who may buy, how payments work and how someone may sell or leave. Brazilian CVM guidance looks at actual rights, not just names such as “utility” or “governance.” [CVM Guidance Opinion 40](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/legislacao/pareceres-orientacao/pare040.html).

We have not set token supply, resident allocation, returns, lock-up periods, fees or a sale date. This paper claims no SEC or MiCA approval. The first housing project can proceed with ordinary agreements if a token adds no useful benefit.

## 9. What becomes digital

We want people to follow money in and out, see agreed decisions and check payments. Blockchain could provide a shared payment record. It does not check the quality of a roof or replace the family’s agreement.

**Arc Network** is the proposed payment network. Its fees use USDC. **Arctide** is a possible token-creation and trading app, with project fees that might help fund work. **Argus and Uniswap** are alternatives. Compare the cost, permissions and support needed for the same job. No universal cheapest option is established. [Arc](https://www.arc.io/litepaper); [Argus](https://github.com/arguspad/argus-world); [Uniswap](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/overview).

**SIAH Protocol is the brand. Hive DAO** names the rules and tools for shared responsibility. People should see what needs doing, choose to take on work and leave useful information for the next person. We want work to continue without constant founder instructions, with room to disagree and ask for review.

For IMOBX, the plan combines Ostrom’s ideas, software agents written in Python, Arc payments and possible Arctide fees. Agents would help keep track of people, wallets, tasks and approvals. **The Flock** is the Telegram bot through which people could ask questions and follow progress. It can serve other projects too.

For example, a resident reports a leak. Someone qualified assesses it, the repair is approved, and someone else checks the work and payment. A replacement should find the information needed to continue. This complete process still needs to be built and demonstrated in IMOBX. [Hive DAO explanation](faq.html#faq-flock-siah).

Families would pay in reais. IMOBX would keep money for Brazilian bills, convert only approved amounts to USDC and convert back to pay builders. CCTP is a possible tool for moving USDC between networks. No conversion provider is confirmed for IMOBX. Residents would not need crypto wallets. [Money path](transparency.html#settlement); [providers researched](transparency.html#ramp-options).

## 10. Pilot roadmap and evidence

The [roadmap](roadmap.html) proposes the first 30 days for roles, property, budget and family discussions; days 31–60 for money and decision rules; and a decision by day 90 on whether the project is ready. Dates start when agreed. Building also needs property, money and permissions.

After families move in, follow at least three months before reviewing expansion. A second local team should be able to repeat the work. The website can report real progress and accounts before any token exists. A later purchase screen would follow agreed rights and a checked legal and payment route.

| What to follow | What to show |
|---|---|
| Homes and agreements | Written property authority and a signed family agreement before moving in. |
| Building | What was delivered, actual cost and any delay. |
| Money | Monthly checks of every receipt, payment and amount set aside. |
| Families | Repair requests, unresolved issues, participation and purchase progress. |
| Continuing the project | Bills covered during low trading and funding for the next homes. |

Agree service targets once the first project’s needs are understood. Token prices and holder counts do not measure how well a family is housed.

## 11. Partner participation

We need practical help: confirm property access, update building and repair budgets, review family agreements and make accounts easy to follow. Families should help shape the service before rules are fixed. This paper asks for expertise and a clear first project, not token purchases.

## 12. Risks, decisions and evidence register

Rafael supplied the November budget and January two-loft account. Their dates and different purposes matter. Designs show intended buildings, not completed homes. No number of donated properties or received funding is confirmed here.

The legal registration, property documents, permissions and signed agreements need checking for the chosen project. Arctide’s documentation describes its service; it does not prove an IMOBX connection is working. Resident voting, reinvestment and token rights remain decisions to agree.

Keep a list of unresolved questions and who will answer them. Update the website and documents as agreements are signed, money arrives and work is checked.
