IMOBX

A documented rent-to-own housing model for Rio

Project paper v0.1 · 15 October 2026 · proposal for founder and partner review

IMOBX is being designed to help households enter a written rent-to-own housing arrangement in Rio de Janeiro. The proposed organization would coordinate property access, building or refurbishment, resident agreements, maintenance and financial reporting. Rafael supplies construction and local operating knowledge; John supplies community and digital coordination. Their contractual roles and the organization's legal form remain to be agreed.

The aim is a repeatable arrangement in which residents can follow their progress toward ownership, partners can see what their money funds, and the operator retains enough cash to maintain homes and prepare the next cohort. The pilot will test whether common agreements and accessible reporting reduce coordination work as participation grows.

The first task is a bounded housing pilot. Digital records and USDC payments are tools to evaluate alongside it. No IMOBX token allocation, tenant-token plan, public offer, property contribution or distribution entitlement is established by this paper. It is a design document, with decisions and evidence needs stated alongside the proposal.

1. Who the pilot is for

The founders want to serve households displaced or excluded from ordinary housing finance who can sustain an affordable payment arrangement. Eligibility, affordability, location, accessibility and residents' preferences need to be defined with local participants. The program should not describe all people in informal settlements as displaced or assume that a move meets every household's needs.

Housing access is a worldwide challenge. UN-Habitat tracks slums, informal settlements and inadequate housing as related categories; local conditions and terminology matter. In Brazil, IBGE describes favelas and urban communities as territories shaped by residents meeting housing and associated needs, with their own community identities. Brazil's 2022 Census recorded approximately 16.4 million residents in these areas, 8.1% of the national population. This national context does not establish conditions or eligibility at a proposed pilot site. IBGE terminology; IBGE 2022 Census; UN-Habitat housing context.

The practical offer to test is a home, a comprehensible agreement, clear maintenance responsibilities and a defined route toward ownership. Residents should understand the payment schedule, what counts toward purchase, how the final price is determined, and what happens after hardship, default, early exit or the operator's failure. Those terms are design decisions for the accountable organization and its Brazilian advisers.

2. The housing and delivery arrangement

The proposed organization would own or hold documented authority to operate each pilot property, sign the resident agreement and contract the builder and other service providers. It would keep a property file covering title or control, encumbrances, permissions, condition, scope, budget and responsibility for delivery and maintenance.

A founder's willingness to provide land or a home is not a completed transfer. The file must distinguish a donation, lease, sale, operating mandate or other contribution. Each has different costs and obligations. A memorandum may record intentions; the chosen legal transfer and registration process determines the property rights. The pilot inventory and completed contribution evidence have not been verified in this publication pass.

Rafael's construction business is a proposed delivery resource. Its catalogue can support its track record when completed work and permissions for publication are checked. Because a founder is associated with the contractor, price, margin, change orders and approval authority should be disclosed and reviewed independently of that founder's own approval.

4. How the money would work

The current direction is to compare a housing-first reinvestment policy against earlier distribution-heavy proposals. No historical percentage split is adopted by this paper.

Every receipt has an identified payer, purpose, permitted use and recipient. The books distinguish initial capital, rent, grants, donations, service receipts, trading fees and resale proceeds. A later buyer's payment to a selling tokenholder is not new construction money for IMOBX; only a separately collected project fee is enterprise income.

The proposed monthly operating sequence is:

  1. Record rent and other collected receipts in the appropriate accounts.
  2. Set aside taxes, collection costs and any restricted amounts.
  3. Pay agreed maintenance, property and administration costs.
  4. Fund reserves for repairs, vacancies, resident obligations and continuity.
  5. Commit growth capital only under the agreed reinvestment and funding rules.
  6. Calculate any legally distributable surplus only after those commitments, if an instrument gives participants that right.

Resident purchase credits are tracked entitlements or obligations under the contract, not a second cash receipt. Reusing received cash must not leave the obligation to complete the home transfer unfunded. Capital invested in exchange liquidity is also different from cash available to build or repair a home.

The accountant should test the full cash cycle rather than a single profitable month. The model must cover a low-trading period, vacancies, cost increases, late payments and the removal of each transferred home from the rental portfolio. Forecasts must show any cash shortfall and its source of funding.

5. Trade fees as supplementary funding

Arctide documents USDC-denominated pool fees with separate liquidity-provider, project and platform components. Its pre-graduation bonding-curve trades have no swap fee. This makes a configured post-launch project-fee recipient a candidate source of supplementary USDC receipts. The actual recipient, allocation and integration would need to be demonstrated for IMOBX. Arctide integration documentation.

Paying from collected USDC does not require issuing new IMOBX tokens for that payment. It still depends on fee-paying market activity. The model should use trade-size and fee-capture assumptions, not trade counts alone, and should show the consequences of low or zero trading. High fees may reduce demand or encourage use of another market.

The proposed $100,000 pilot treasury is a scenario, not committed funding. Funding a home requires spendable cash, permitted uses and a budget; token market value and locked liquidity do not supply that cash by themselves.

6. What self-sufficiency means

IMOBX would be operating self-sufficiently when recurring, lawfully usable income covers recurring property and organization costs and the reserves required to meet resident commitments. Continued expansion additionally requires capital for new homes. These are separate financial tests.

A home transferred to its resident stops being a rental-income asset for the operator. The model therefore needs to identify what remains: for example, a retained portfolio, paid services, a contractually assigned share of completed external construction profits or another recurring source. None is assumed to exist until agreed and received. A servicing charge must correspond to a real service, be affordable and respect the resident's bargain.

Grants and donations can support an eligible pilot or expansion. Their restrictions must remain visible; a one-time award is not recurring operating income. Claiming self-sufficiency at 1,000 homes is premature until a cohort model includes build timing, costs, tenancy terms, transfers and asset runoff. Three- and six-year reviews are possible checkpoints, not dates at which distributions automatically begin.

7. Resident voice and accountable governance

The long-term ambition is a DAO with shared participation rules and useful digital records, built around an accountable housing organization. Its practical rules should emerge from the pilot and resident consultation.

The founders want to apply Ostrom's work where it fits: define the participating group, involve affected users in suitable decisions, make monitoring and dispute resolution accessible, and connect local decisions with wider organization responsibilities. This is a design direction, not a claim that token voting implements those principles. Ostrom's Nobel lecture.

The starting consultation should include residents, prospective residents, the builder, the organization, local service partners and any funders. They should agree which decisions each group can make and which rights cannot be changed by a simple vote.

Area Proposed decision boundary to review
A resident's occupancy and ownership path Governed by their agreement and applicable law; protected from unrelated token votes.
Local maintenance priorities and shared facilities Resident participation within an agreed budget and safety responsibilities.
Property transfer and contractor payments Responsible legal officers under documented controls and conflict rules.
Enterprise budgets and expansion Defined board/member authority, reporting and resident representation.
Any investor rights Exact instrument terms and legal register; separate from resident eligibility.

Longer residence may provide experience relevant to an elected role. It should not silently change a purchase agreement or remove newer residents' basic voice. A household/member voting approach and separate resident representation are options for consultation; they are not settled allocations. No tenant-token plan has yet been adopted.

8. What a token could represent

The founders’ current research direction includes a possible yield-bearing token: specified rights to any future distributable surplus after housing obligations, operating costs and required reserves. Its rights, income sources and lawful structure remain to be designed; no current payout entitlement is established.

The token decision follows the housing, cash and governance design. Options include a nontransferable participation credential or a legally structured financial interest. They serve different purposes and should not be promoted as interchangeable. A supporter credential does not imply equity, property title or rental income.

If the desired product includes an economic claim, the issuer, rights, offer, eligibility, transfers and distribution method need to be specified with Brazilian advisers and a suitable provider. CVM guidance considers a cryptoasset's characteristics and rights when determining the relevant treatment; its label does not settle the question. CVM Guidance Opinion 40.

The paper does not set token supply, tenant allocation, staking periods, dividends, fees or a public-sale date. It also does not claim SEC or MiCA approval. Foreign access would need its own assessment. If token participation adds no useful right or administrative benefit, the housing pilot can proceed with ordinary records and contracts.

9. What becomes digital

The planned blockchain work focuses on traceable treasury receipts, authorized distributions and governance records within agreed decision boundaries. The complete token model remains in research and development, including rights, resident participation, distribution and transfer rules. These are proposed implementation areas, not a deployed IMOBX treasury or token system.

The proposed digital service would help people find approved project information, read progress reports and obtain support. The Flock is the candidate Telegram access point for questions, resident-support handoffs, community education and approved updates. It remains a neutral service able to support other projects and compatible providers.

Any confirmed contribution record or contributor reward requires the authorized backend service and a funded, approved work program. These capabilities are proposed requirements here. The website and bot must not imply that posting, recruiting token buyers or owning a token automatically earns money.

USDC settlement could make authorized transfers and receipts easier to inspect across counterparties. Brazilian commitments remain budgeted in BRL; conversion rates, costs and currency risk need reconciliation. Personal resident records, identity documents and private agreements should not be placed on a public ledger. The digital interface must provide a practical route for residents who do not use crypto wallets.

10. Pilot roadmap and evidence

The proposed sequence is: founder agreement and property file; selected home and resident terms; budget and cash model; governance/legal/provider decisions; reviewed public paper and website; funded delivery and reporting; then a decision on further homes and any token trial.

Three work streams may advance together: Rafael's housing and contractor evidence; John's communications and website; and professional review of the organization, resident rights and funding arrangement. Construction dates depend on the selected property, funding, permits and contractor plan. No token date is committed.

Pilot measure Proposed evidence or decision
Property and resident rights A documented control arrangement and executed resident agreement for every included home.
Budget and delivery Current all-in budget, completion evidence, actual cost and schedule variance.
Financial reporting Monthly reconciliation of all receipts, payments, restrictions and reserves.
Resident experience Accessible support, maintenance response record, unresolved issues and recorded participation.
Ownership progress Progress according to the signed contract, including any purchase credit and eventual transfer.
Continuity and expansion Costed operation through a low-trading case and funded capacity for the next cohort.

Numeric service and reserve targets should be agreed after the pilot's baseline is known. Public reporting should distinguish planned, contracted, paid, delivered and independently checked results. Tokenholder counts and trading volume are not housing impact measures.

11. Partner participation

The next partnership work is specific: validate property access, update cost and maintenance budgets, review resident agreements, test the funding/legal route and design understandable reporting. A small resident consultation group can help shape the service before rules are fixed.

The first partner deck asks for expertise, evidence and a defined pilot delivery scope. It does not solicit investment or direct people to buy a token. A later funding proposal should state the selected vehicle, amount, exact uses, restrictions, responsibilities and reporting obligations only when established.

12. Risks, decisions and evidence register

Material questions remain: property contribution/control, legal form, enforceable resident terms, current costs, builder conflicts, affordability, reserves, tenancy risks, construction delivery, digital support, fraud, custody, conversion, market liquidity and permitted token transfers. The controls and responsible people should be named for the selected pilot, rather than assumed from the use of a DAO.
