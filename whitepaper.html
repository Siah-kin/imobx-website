IMOBX

A rent-to-own housing proposal for Rio

Project paper · Version 0.7, 21 October 2026

IMOBX is preparing a rent-to-own housing project in Rio de Janeiro. A family rents a home, part of each payment counts towards buying it, and the whole home is transferred to the family once the agreed conditions are met. The plan brings together local building work, written agreements, maintenance and accounts people can check.

What exists today. Rafael’s November 2025 village budget; his January 2026 report on a two-loft contract; drawings; a village illustration made with an image generator; and construction photographs supplied by the team. The drawings and photographs are not a listing of available homes.

What is not decided. The property. The home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay, and how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Repairs, missed payments and leaving. IMOBX’s legal form, and Rafael’s and John’s responsibilities, which need written agreements. Funding and what funders receive. The rights of any token, which is a digital record of a right that can be bought and sold.

Rafael describes his construction experience and knowledge of local conditions; his references are not yet listed. John works on community communication and digital tools.

Under the plan, a family rents the home and makes monthly payments in reais. The agreement records the amount credited towards the purchase and the remaining agreed price. Ownership of the whole home passes to the household once the purchase conditions and property transfer are completed. IMOBX then stops receiving rent from that home.

The household explanation covers payments, repairs, decisions and leaving in Portuguese and English. The dated project reference identifies sources and current responsibilities; its chapter map connects the available material to Rafael’s longer manuscript.

1. Who the pilot is for

The first project is for families who have been displaced or cannot use ordinary housing finance. Who can join, what they can afford and which locations meet their needs are still to be agreed with local participants. A move has to make sense for the family’s work, school, transport and support network.

Housing problems exist worldwide; UN-Habitat’s page on adequate housing gives general background and does not describe any IMOBX family. In Brazil, favelas and urban communities have their own histories, businesses and relationships. IBGE’s terminology page explains the term “favelas and urban communities”. The 2022 Census release counted around 16.4 million residents in these areas, 8.1% of the population. That national figure does not tell us the condition of a particular home or family. Living in a favela does not itself mean someone is displaced.

How a household moves from renting to owning

A family takes three steps towards owning the home. First, IMOBX and the household agree the home, purchase price, monthly payment and amount credited towards purchase. Then each payment updates the household’s purchase record. Finally, once the purchase conditions are met, the required documents and registration transfer the whole home to the household.

The purchase record shows the total paid and the part of that money that counts towards buying the home. Only that agreed part reduces the price still owed. It is part of the same payment, not extra income for IMOBX. Neither the village construction budget nor the separate two-loft contract is an announced sale price for a household.

Repair charges, late payments, temporary hardship and leaving the arrangement need their own agreed terms, and none is set. No refund formula or grace period has been selected. Before moving in, a household needs to know how the amounts already counted towards buying the home are treated if it leaves or if IMOBX cannot continue. These are open terms of the first offer, not decisions already made on the household’s behalf. See payments and transfer and hardship and leaving.

2. The housing and delivery arrangement

Under the plan, IMOBX owns each included property or holds a written legal right to operate it. It signs agreements with families and hires builders and other providers. Each property needs a file showing its owner, debts or restrictions, permissions, condition, building plan, budget and responsible people.

A property can reach IMOBX by donation, lease, sale or another agreement, and the property’s agreement says which. A letter saying someone intends to donate is different from a completed transfer. Ownership needs the required property registration.

One structure under consideration is a foreign organization holding Brazilian property. Receita Federal’s guidance for legal entities based abroad confirms that such an organization needs a CNPJ, Brazil’s business tax registration. A CNPJ does not give property ownership or make a bank or exchange accept the organization; these are separate matters. IMOBX has not chosen its legal form and no property is confirmed here, so both still need documents.

Rafael’s building company is a contractor under consideration. Its catalogue can show experience once the completed work, and permission to publish it, have been checked. Under the plan, a founder who is connected to a builder does not approve that builder’s bill. Someone else checks the price, profit, changes and completed work.

Rafael's village vision, 30 November 2025

Rafael’s vision is 45 m² two-level homes facing a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The aim is a path to ownership, lower household costs and places to meet and learn. The illustration shows this vision, not a completed village, and it was made with an image generator.

Under the plan, residents agree who cares for shared spaces, how study rooms are used and how repairs are paid for. Solar savings depend on real equipment and usage figures, and some electricity charges remain. ANEEL’s distributed-generation guidance sets out the rules for small solar systems; it gives no figures for these homes.

Rafael prepared a US$27,777 budget per 45 m² home using CUB and SINAPI, Brazilian building-cost references, and his purchasing and construction plan. On 30 November 2025, he showed a US$21,883.86 construction calculation plus US$5,550 for land. He then supplied this revised budget:

His plan calls for at least 36 homes built together: US$999,972 over 12 months. Buying directly and sharing building work and facilities are part of the savings plan. Each actual build must record its price date, quantities, included work, costs in reais and invoices.

These are Rafael’s project figures, not a finished-home price published by a cost index. SINAPI is the Caixa reference for construction costs and inputs; a specific budget still needs its own location, date and scope. CUB leaves out some site works, connections, design and builder payments, as the CUB scope page explains. A saving has to be explained through the work and purchasing plan; deducting a presumed profit percentage does not prove it.

The separate two-loft case

On 20 January 2026, Rafael reported a contract for two lofts, waiting for the first deposit. The construction figure is US$27,777 for both lofts together: US$13,888.50 each by division. The included work, current progress and final costs are not yet documented. This is separate from the village budget. See the drawings and photos.

For the first project, each family’s agreement needs affordable payments in reais and clear rules for price changes. The payment amount and purchase term are open, as the status at the top of this paper says.

4. How the money would work

The financial plan covers homes and continued service. The rules for payment and reinvestment are not agreed.

The accounts identify each payer, purpose and permitted use, with separate records for rent, donations, grants, investments, service income and trading fees. If someone buys a token from another holder, the sale money normally goes to the seller. Only a separately collected project fee goes to IMOBX.

Under the plan, money is used in this order:

  1. Record the money actually received.
  2. Set aside taxes and amounts promised for a specific purpose.
  3. Pay agreed property, maintenance and administration bills.
  4. Keep money for repairs, empty homes, promises made to families and unexpected costs.
  5. Fund new homes under the agreed budget.
  6. Pay investors only if money remains and a valid agreement allows it.

Payments credited toward a family’s purchase are a commitment under its agreement. They are not a second receipt of cash. Using the money elsewhere must not make the promised home transfer impossible. Money locked in a trading market also cannot pay a building bill at the same time.

5. Trade fees as supplementary funding

The token proposal includes financing branding and outreach. A community of supporters introduces the project to prospective residents, owners, partners and funders. Construction, outreach and any investor payments have separate budgets and obligations.

A fee collected when people buy or sell the token is an optional source of funding. IMOBX needs an agreement stating which fees it receives, what they can pay for and how receipts are checked. A sale between two holders normally pays the seller; it does not provide a new building budget for IMOBX.

These receipts can help fund agreed work without creating more IMOBX tokens for that payment. They still depend on trading, so the plan is tested with little or no trading income. Higher fees may discourage buyers and sellers.

The construction budget needs committed funding. A token’s market value does not establish money available to pay building bills. Payments for accepted outreach work come from a funded campaign budget. Eligibility, review, compensation disclosure and any point-redemption terms are defined before the programme pays participants.

6. What self-sufficiency means

Self-sufficiency has two separate tests. The first is whether recurring income covers IMOBX’s regular bills, repairs and the cost of keeping promises to families, which keeps the existing operation going. The second is building more homes, which needs additional money.

When a family becomes the owner, IMOBX stops receiving rent from that home. Forecasts must show how income continues afterward. Any ongoing service charge needs a real service, an affordable price and terms the resident agreed to.

A one-off grant can help build, but it is not continuing income. Having 1,000 homes does not by itself show that the accounts balance. We need to follow groups of homes from building through payments to ownership.

7. Resident voice and accountable governance

We are developing a DAO: an organization where people share decisions and use software to help follow the agreed rules. Residents, property contributors, builders, staff, donors, investors, neighbours and independent checkers need clear roles. We have mapped their likely needs for discussion, not confirmed them through interviews.

Elinor Ostrom studied how people manage shared resources, and her Nobel lecture is the research basis for the principles we use. Applying them to IMOBX is the project’s own step; the lecture does not endorse IMOBX. We apply these lessons: define the group, involve affected people, check work and spending, resolve disputes and keep local decisions close to residents.

Who or what Rule under discussion
Families Affordable payments, repairs and a written path to owning the whole home.
Property contributors Clear donation, sale or lease terms and responsibility for debts and transfer costs.
Builders and suppliers Agreed work, price, payment dates and independent checks of the completed work.
Staff and resident representatives Clear powers, spending limits, appointment, reporting and replacement.
Donors and social funders Agreed use of funds and evidence of housing results.
Possible investors Written rights stating what investors would receive, costs, risk of loss, decision powers and how they sell or leave.
Neighbours A voice on local impacts, services and access.
Independent checkers Access and authority to review property, safety, accounts and agreements.

Examples of resident decisions are study-room hours, courtyard use and the order of shared-space repairs. One option under discussion is one vote per member on these matters, with representatives who can be replaced. The International Cooperative Alliance’s statement of cooperative identity describes democratic member control; it does not mean IMOBX is a registered cooperative. People handling maintenance act within an agreed budget, and someone else checks the work and bill. A resident who disagrees with a charge can ask for it to be checked.

A vote, including a token vote, cannot on its own change a family’s housing agreement. Buying an investment token does not buy control over another household’s purchase agreement. Residents’ say does not depend on investing, promotional work or online activity points. Investors receive only the rights written in their agreement. The voting procedure, spending limits, repair responsibilities and review process are not designed yet; they are developed with participants. See resident decisions and repairs and decision examples.

8. What a token could represent

The reason to consider a token is to finance IMOBX’s operations, branding and outreach, with a community helping introduce the housing project to partners and supporters. When investors buy new tokens, they pay the organisation that creates and sells them, and that organisation uses the money under the agreed funding terms. What buyers receive has not been chosen. A later sale between holders normally pays the seller; any fee received by IMOBX is recorded separately.

Four paths are under study:

Path What a participant receives Main decision still open
A. No IMOBX token A housing agreement, or payment for completed work that meets the agreement. Funding sources, campaign budget and work-review rules.
B. Service or membership benefit A specific service or discount under stated terms. The service, who funds discounts and any separately funded buyback.
C. Share of collected fees A written right to receive part of the fees actually collected, if legally permitted. Who creates the token, what buyers receive, costs, restrictions, risk of loss and the legal process for selling it.
D. Investment in the operation Shares recorded as tokens, or another written right stating what the investor receives. The organisation responsible, the property and debts included, investor rights and the legal process for selling the investment.

These paths give different rights. A buyback pays a seller; burning the purchased tokens does not automatically pay remaining holders. Dividend rights require their own structure. Reinvesting income, sometimes called compounding, requires money actually received and a rule explaining its use. Locking tokens, called staking, does not create that income.

The outreach programme sets out tasks in writing, checks the work submitted and pays for work that meets the agreement from money already set aside. A community tool can show the task, who checked the work and whether it was paid for. Housing rights and residents’ voice do not depend on this programme.

Any later payment of profits or rental income depends on the chosen agreement. The agreement has to say which income can be paid to investors and which bills and promises are covered first. No date has been agreed for payments of profits or rental income to token holders. Money raised from funders, fees collected from token trading and income from running the homes have separate records. Investors receive only the rights written in their agreement.

The plan is that each family owns its whole home. Sharing purchasing and administration across projects can save work. The proposal does not split any house between token buyers. An investment linked to IMOBX or to several projects has to state which properties, debts, income and shared risks it includes.

The nonprofit idea is meant to keep money serving housing, and IMOBX’s legal form is not decided. Law 13.019, which covers partnerships with the public sector, defines nonprofits as organisations that use their results for their social purpose (article 2(I)(a)). The law does not say how such an organisation can offer investments, and we cannot simply promise those results as investor dividends. Brazilian advisers need to review participation without profit rights and any other permitted investment arrangement. A donation given for building homes keeps its agreed purpose.

A membership record and an investment do different jobs. A membership record does not itself give property ownership or rental income. If an investment is offered, its agreement must say who owes what, who can buy, how payments work and how someone can sell or leave. Brazil’s securities-market regulator, the CVM, looks at actual rights, not just names such as “utility” or “governance”, in its Guidance Opinion 40. That opinion explains how the CVM assesses tokens; it does not approve any IMOBX offer.

The token model and its rights remain under research. Promoters need to disclose compensation and follow the rules of each channel and country reached. No utility or governance label settles the legal assessment. This paper claims no regulatory approval. The first housing project can go ahead with ordinary agreements if a token adds no useful benefit. See the current alternatives and sources.

9. What becomes digital

Under the plan, people can follow money in and out, see agreed decisions and check payments. A blockchain is a shared digital record that shows who approved a payment and when, and it can hold the payment record. It does not check the quality of a roof or replace the family’s agreement.

Arctide and Argus are examples of exchanges, or trading platforms, on the Arc network, a blockchain. Their sites show that such platforms exist; they do not show any IMOBX use or partnership.

For the first project, we compare a shared digital record with ordinary accounts and signed agreements: what gets easier to check, how much it costs, who can correct an error and who keeps access. A token cannot create rental income, verify a building or guarantee a buyer when someone wants to leave. The OECD’s study of tokenising assets in financial markets is general background on tokenisation; it does not review IMOBX.

Residents and the organization agree who decides each matter, spending limits and how a decision can be reviewed. Everyday work follows these rules without waiting for instructions from the founder.

For example, a resident reports a leak through the support channel. The person handling maintenance assesses it, gets a quote and follows the agreed spending rule. Someone else checks the repair. The payment and receipt go into the accounts. Residents can raise a problem and ask for review.

Tasks need a responsible person, deadline and budget. If someone leaves a role, their replacement finds the open work, approvals and documents needed to continue. Residents need ways to choose and replace responsible people. Personal information stays private.

Software assistants can organize documents and remind people about unfinished work, and blockchain records can show payment amounts and destinations. The agreed rules still determine who decides or spends. This method has not been tested: it needs agreement with participants and a working pilot. See daily decisions and tasks.

Families pay in reais. IMOBX keeps money for Brazilian bills and uses digital payments only for an agreed purpose. Any conversion needs an accepted provider, a full cost record and a tested route back to the bank. No provider is confirmed for IMOBX. Residents do not need crypto wallets. See the money path and the payment-service requirements.

10. Pilot roadmap and evidence

The roadmap follows six stages: legal structure and property, resident participation, first homes, payment records people can check, funding and account review, and further projects. Each stage explains who does the work, what must happen first and which records are needed before it proceeds. Construction dates depend on the chosen project, funding and permissions. Legal preparation, resident discussions and digital trials can progress alongside one another.

After families move in, the plan follows at least three months before reviewing expansion. A second local team should be able to repeat the work. The website can report real progress and accounts before any token exists. A later purchase screen follows agreed rights and a checked legal and payment route.

What to follow What to show
Homes and agreements Written proof of the right to use the property and a signed family agreement before moving in.
Building What was delivered, actual cost and any delay.
Money Monthly checks of all money received, paid and set aside.
Families Repair requests, unresolved issues, participation and purchase progress.
Continuing the project Bills covered during low trading and funding for the next homes.

Service targets depend on the first project’s needs and resident participation. An unresolved property right, funding gap or unsafe building prevents the affected stage from proceeding. Expansion requires evidence that there is enough money for promises already made. Token prices and holder counts do not measure how well a family is housed.

11. Partner participation

The participation page has separate routes for residents, property owners, building professionals, community organisations, funding partners and community contributors. A household can describe its location and housing needs. An owner can describe a property and whether donation, sale or lease is being considered. A builder can introduce a service and area of work. A funding partner can state the type of support and documents it needs to assess.

Receiving contacts are not yet available. Visitors can prepare and copy an introduction, but the site does not send it or reserve housing, appoint a contractor or accept an investment. Routes: household, property and funding.

The partnership under discussion combines IMOBX as project organiser, OLYMPUS as a possible builder, linked to Rafael, and a community or charitable organisation as a possible property or local partner. A financing partner can support an agreed project budget. These relationships are proposals, with no partner agreement or funding commitment established here. A property visit can produce a condition assessment and a costed next step.

Builders, legal advisers and accountants review their own areas. Families help shape the service before rules are fixed. If a builder is connected to an IMOBX founder, other people must compare prices, approve the contract and inspect the completed work. See people and appointment status.

12. Risks and what still needs checking

Rafael supplied the November budget and January two-loft account. Their dates and different purposes matter. Designs show intended buildings, not completed homes. No number of donated properties or received funding is confirmed here.

The legal registration, property documents, permissions and signed agreements need checking for the chosen project. The researched payment services do not establish a working IMOBX connection. Resident voting, reinvestment and token rights are decisions still to be agreed.

The public risk list identifies construction, household, cash, offering and software risks, with reviewing roles still to be appointed. The complete financial model is not ready yet: it needs site costs in reais, affordable payments, repair and delay scenarios, and zero token-trading income. This paper does not show that income covers all costs or that the project can work across Brazil.

The CVM’s Resolution 249 of 13 October 2026 creates an experimental programme for distributed-ledger technology, the technical name for shared digital records such as a blockchain. Article 1, paragraph 3 excludes real investors, actually issued securities and real-money movements in those tests; it does not authorise a public offering. The project’s legal form and any way to raise the money need their own review.

Project updates show agreements signed, funding received and work completed, alongside open questions and responsible roles. The source list shows which statement each source supports. The version record identifies this edition for citation; private household documents stay unpublished.
