IMOBX

A documented rent-to-own housing model for Rio

Project paper v0.7 · 19 October 2026 · Project proposal

IMOBX wants to help families rent a home and follow a clear path to buying it. We are preparing a first housing project in Rio de Janeiro. The plan brings together local building work, written agreements, maintenance and accounts people can check.

Rafael brings construction experience and knowledge of local conditions. John works on community communication and digital tools. Their responsibilities and IMOBX’s legal form still need to be agreed in writing.

A family would rent the home and make monthly payments in reais. The agreement would record the amount credited towards the purchase and the remaining agreed price. Ownership of the whole home would pass to the household once the purchase conditions and property transfer are completed. IMOBX would then stop receiving rent from that home.

For the first project, the home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay have not been set. How much of each payment would count towards buying the home also remains to be agreed. The household explanation covers the proposed payments, repairs, decisions and exit questions in Portuguese and English. The dated project reference identifies sources and current responsibilities; its chapter map connects the available material to Rafael’s longer manuscript.

1. Who the pilot is for

The first project would serve families who have been displaced or cannot use ordinary housing finance. With local participants, we need to agree who can join, what they can afford and which locations meet their needs. A move must make sense for the family’s work, school, transport and support network.

Housing problems exist worldwide. In Brazil, favelas and urban communities have their own histories, businesses and relationships. Brazil’s 2022 Census counted around 16.4 million residents in these areas, 8.1% of the population. That national figure does not tell us the condition of a particular home or family. Living in a favela does not itself mean someone is displaced. IBGE terminology; 2022 Census; UN-Habitat.

How a household would move from renting to owning

A family would take three steps towards owning the home. First, IMOBX and the household agree the home, purchase price, monthly payment and amount credited towards purchase. Then each payment updates the household’s purchase record. Finally, once the purchase conditions are met, the required documents and registration transfer the whole home to the household.

The purchase record would show the total paid and the part of that money that counts towards buying the home. Only that agreed part reduces the price still owed. It is part of the same payment, not extra income for IMOBX. Neither the village construction budget nor the separate two-loft contract is an announced sale price for a household.

Repair charges, late payments, temporary hardship and leaving the arrangement need their own agreed terms. No refund formula or grace period has been selected. A household would need to know how amounts previously counted towards buying the home is treated if it leaves or IMOBX cannot continue. These are open terms of the first offer, not decisions already made on the household’s behalf. Payments and transfer; hardship and leaving.

2. The housing and delivery arrangement

IMOBX would own each included property or have a written legal right to operate it. It would sign agreements with families and hire builders and other providers. Each property needs a file showing its owner, debts or restrictions, permissions, condition, building plan, budget and responsible people.

A property might be donated, leased, sold or made available under another agreement. Its agreement needs to identify which arrangement applies. A letter saying someone intends to donate is different from a completed transfer. Ownership needs the required property registration.

July’s discussions considered a foreign organization holding Brazilian property. Receita Federal confirms that such an organization needs a CNPJ, Brazil’s business tax registration. Registration, property ownership and acceptance by a bank or exchange are separate matters. IMOBX’s chosen legal form and any received properties still need documents. Receita Federal.

Rafael’s building company is a possible contractor. Its catalogue can show experience when completed work and permission to publish it are checked. If a founder is connected to a builder, that founder should not approve the builder’s bill. Someone else needs to check the price, profit, changes and completed work.

Rafael's village vision, 30 November 2025

Rafael proposed 45 m² two-level homes facing a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The aim is a path to ownership, lower household costs and places to meet and learn. The illustration shows this vision, not a completed village.

Residents would help agree who cares for shared spaces, how study rooms are used and how repairs are paid for. Solar savings need real equipment and usage figures; some electricity charges remain. ANEEL guidance.

4. How the money would work

The proposed financial plan covers homes and continued service. Payment and reinvestment rules remain to be agreed.

The proposed accounts would identify each payer, purpose and permitted use, with separate records for rent, donations, grants, investments, service income and trading fees. If someone buys a token from another holder, the sale money normally goes to the seller. Only a separately collected project fee goes to IMOBX.

The proposed order is:

  1. Record the money actually received.
  2. Set aside taxes and amounts promised for a specific purpose.
  3. Pay agreed property, maintenance and administration bills.
  4. Keep money for repairs, empty homes, promises made to families and unexpected costs.
  5. Fund new homes under the agreed budget.
  6. Consider investor payments only if money remains and a valid agreement allows them.

Payments credited toward a family’s purchase are a commitment under its agreement. They are not a second receipt of cash. Using the money elsewhere must not make the promised home transfer impossible. Money locked in a trading market also cannot pay a building bill at the same time.

5. Trade fees as supplementary funding

The token proposal includes financing branding and outreach. A community of supporters could introduce the project to prospective residents, owners, partners and funders. Construction, outreach and any investor payments have separate budgets and obligations.

An optional source of funding is a fee collected when people buy or sell a proposed token. IMOBX would need an agreement stating which fees it receives, what they may pay for and how receipts are checked. A sale between two holders normally pays the seller; it does not provide a new building budget for IMOBX.

These receipts could help fund agreed work without creating more IMOBX tokens for that payment. They still depend on trading. Test the plan with little or no trading income; higher fees may discourage buyers and sellers.

The construction budget needs committed funding. A token’s market value does not establish money available to pay building bills. Payments for accepted outreach work would come from a funded campaign budget. Eligibility, review, compensation disclosure and any point-redemption terms need to be defined before the programme pays participants.

6. What self-sufficiency means

IMOBX would support its existing operation when recurring income covers its regular bills, repairs and the cost of keeping promises to families. Building more homes needs additional money. These are two separate tests.

When a family becomes the owner, IMOBX stops receiving rent from that home. Forecasts must show how income continues afterward. Any ongoing service charge needs a real service, an affordable price and terms the resident agreed to.

A one-off grant can help build, but it is not continuing income. Having 1,000 homes does not by itself show that the accounts balance. We need to follow groups of homes from building through payments to ownership.

7. Resident voice and accountable governance

We are developing a DAO: an organization where people share decisions and use software to help follow the agreed rules. Residents, property contributors, builders, staff, donors, possible investors, neighbours and independent checkers need clear roles. We have mapped their likely needs for discussion, not confirmed them through interviews.

Ostrom studied how people manage shared resources. We want to apply useful lessons: define the group, involve affected people, check work and spending, resolve disputes and keep local decisions close to residents. Ostrom’s lecture.

Who or what Proposed rule to discuss
Families Affordable payments, repairs and a written path to owning the whole home.
Property contributors Clear donation, sale or lease terms and responsibility for debts and transfer costs.
Builders and suppliers Agreed work, price, payment dates and independent checks of the completed work.
Staff and resident representatives Clear powers, spending limits, appointment, reporting and replacement.
Donors and social funders Agreed use of funds and evidence of housing results.
Possible investors Written rights explaining what investors could receive, costs, possible losses, decision powers and how they could sell or leave.
Neighbours A voice on local impacts, services and access.
Independent checkers Access and authority to review property, safety, accounts and agreements.

For example, residents could agree study-room hours, courtyard use and the order of shared-space repairs. One option under discussion is one vote per member on these matters, with representatives who can be replaced. People handling maintenance would act within an agreed budget; someone else would check the work and bill. A resident who disagrees with a charge would have a way to ask for it to be checked.

Buying an investment token would not buy control over another household’s purchase agreement. Participation in resident decisions would be independent of investment, promotional work and online activity points. The voting procedure, spending limits, repair responsibilities and review process remain to be developed with participants. Resident decisions and repairs; cooperative principles; decision examples.

8. What a token could represent

The motivation includes financing IMOBX’s operations, branding and outreach, with a community helping introduce the housing project to partners and supporters. When investors buy new tokens, they would pay the organisation that creates and sells them. That organisation would use the money under the agreed funding terms. What buyers would receive has not been chosen. A later sale between holders normally pays the seller; any fee received by IMOBX would be recorded separately.

Four paths remain under study:

Path What a participant would receive Main decision still open
A. No IMOBX token A housing agreement, or payment for completed work that meets the agreement. Funding sources, campaign budget and work-review rules.
B. Service or membership benefit A specific service or discount under stated terms. The service, who funds discounts and any separately funded buyback.
C. Share of collected fees A written right to receive part of the fees actually collected, if legally permitted. Who would create the token, what buyers could receive, costs, restrictions, losses and the legal process for selling it.
D. Investment in the operation Shares recorded as tokens, or another written right stating what the investor could receive. The organisation responsible, the property and debts included, investor rights and the legal process for selling the investment.

These paths give different rights. A buyback pays a seller; burning the purchased tokens does not automatically pay remaining holders. Dividend rights require their own structure. Reinvesting income, sometimes called compounding, requires money actually received and a rule explaining its use. Locking tokens, called staking, does not create that income.

The outreach programme would set out tasks in writing, check the work submitted and pay for work that meets the agreement from money already set aside. A community tool could show the task, who checked the work and whether it had been paid for. Housing rights and residents’ voice would remain independent of this programme.

Any later payments of profits or rental income would depend on the chosen agreement. It would need to say which income may be paid to investors and which bills and promises must be covered first. No date has been agreed for payments of profits or rental income to token holders. Money raised from funders, fees collected from token trading and income from running the homes need separate records.

We want each family to be able to own its whole home. Sharing purchasing and administration across projects could save work. The proposal does not split every house between token buyers. A possible investment linked to IMOBX or several projects would need to state which properties, debts, income and shared risks it includes.

The nonprofit idea is intended to keep money serving housing. Its legal form remains undecided. Law 13.019, covering public-sector partnerships, defines nonprofits that use their results for their social purpose. We cannot simply promise those results as investor dividends. Brazilian advisers need to review participation without profit rights and any other permitted investment arrangement. A donation given for building homes must keep its agreed purpose. Law 13.019, article 2(I)(a).

A membership record and an investment do different jobs. A membership record does not itself give property ownership or rental income. If an investment is offered, its agreement must say who owes what, who may buy, how payments work and how someone may sell or leave. Brazilian CVM guidance looks at actual rights, not just names such as “utility” or “governance.” CVM Guidance Opinion 40.

The token model and its rights remain under research. Promoters would need to disclose compensation and follow the rules of each channel and country reached. No utility or governance label settles the legal assessment. This paper claims no regulatory approval. The first housing project can proceed with ordinary agreements if a token adds no useful benefit. Current alternatives and sources.

9. What becomes digital

We want people to follow money in and out, see agreed decisions and check payments. Blockchain could provide a shared payment record. It does not check the quality of a roof or replace the family’s agreement.

Arctide and Argus are examples of exchanges on the Arc network.

For the first project, compare a shared digital record with ordinary accounts and signed agreements: what gets easier to check, how much does it cost, who can correct an error and who keeps access? A token cannot create rental income, verify a building or guarantee a buyer when someone wants to leave. OECD study.

Residents and the organization would agree who decides each matter, spending limits and how a decision can be reviewed. Everyday work should follow these rules without waiting for instructions from the founder.

For example, a resident reports a leak through the support channel. The person handling maintenance assesses it, gets a quote and follows the agreed spending rule. Someone else checks the repair. The payment and receipt go into the accounts. Residents can raise a problem and ask for review.

Tasks need a responsible person, deadline and budget. If someone leaves a role, their replacement should find the open work, approvals and documents needed to continue. Residents need ways to choose and replace responsible people. Personal information stays private.

Software assistants could organize documents and remind people about unfinished work. Blockchain records could show payment amounts and destinations. The agreed rules still determine who may decide or spend. This method needs agreement with participants and a working pilot. Daily decisions and tasks.

Families would pay in reais. IMOBX would keep money for Brazilian bills and use digital payments only for an agreed purpose. Any conversion needs an accepted provider, a full cost record and a tested route back to the bank. No provider is confirmed for IMOBX. Residents would not need crypto wallets. Money path; payment-service requirements.

10. Pilot roadmap and evidence

The roadmap follows six stages: legal structure and property, resident participation, first homes, payment records people can check, funding and account review, and further projects. Each stage explains who would do the work, what must happen first and which records are needed before it can proceed. Construction dates depend on the chosen project, funding and permissions. Legal preparation, resident discussions and digital trials can progress alongside one another.

After families move in, follow at least three months before reviewing expansion. A second local team should be able to repeat the work. The website can report real progress and accounts before any token exists. A later purchase screen would follow agreed rights and a checked legal and payment route.

What to follow What to show
Homes and agreements Written proof of the right to use the property and a signed family agreement before moving in.
Building What was delivered, actual cost and any delay.
Money Monthly checks of all money received, paid and set aside.
Families Repair requests, unresolved issues, participation and purchase progress.
Continuing the project Bills covered during low trading and funding for the next homes.

Service targets depend on the first project’s needs and resident participation. An unresolved property right, funding gap or unsafe building would prevent the affected stage from proceeding. Expansion requires evidence that there is enough money for promises already made. Token prices and holder counts do not measure how well a family is housed.

11. Partner participation

The participation page has separate routes for residents, property owners, building professionals, community organisations, funding partners and community contributors. A household can describe its location and housing needs. An owner can describe a property and whether donation, sale or lease is being considered. A builder can introduce a service and area of work. A funding partner can state the type of support and documents it needs to assess.

Receiving contacts are not yet available. Visitors can prepare and copy an introduction, but the site does not send it or reserve housing, appoint a contractor or accept an investment. Household route; property route; funding route.

A possible partnership would combine IMOBX as project organiser, OLYMPUS as a contracted builder and a community or charitable organisation as a property or local partner. A financing partner could support an agreed project budget. These are proposed relationships, with no partner agreement or funding commitment established here. A property visit could produce a condition assessment and a costed next step.

Builders, legal advisers and accountants would review their own areas. Families would help shape the service before rules are fixed. If a builder is connected to an IMOBX founder, other people must compare prices, approve the contract and inspect the completed work. People and appointment status.

12. Risks and what still needs checking

Rafael supplied the November budget and January two-loft account. Their dates and different purposes matter. Designs show intended buildings, not completed homes. No number of donated properties or received funding is confirmed here.

The legal registration, property documents, permissions and signed agreements need checking for the chosen project. The researched payment services do not establish a working IMOBX connection. Resident voting, reinvestment and token rights remain decisions to agree.

The public risk list identifies construction, household, cash, offering and software risks, with reviewing roles still to be appointed. The complete financial model is not ready yet: it needs site costs in reais, affordable payments, repair and delay scenarios, and zero token-trading income. This paper does not show that income would cover all costs or that the project could work across Brazil.

CVM is Brazil’s securities-market regulator. Its Resolution 249 creates an experimental DLT programme. Article 1, paragraph 3 excludes real investors, actually issued securities and real-money movements in those tests; it does not authorise a public offering. The project’s legal form and any way to raise the money need their own review. CVM Resolution 249.

Project updates would show agreements signed, funding received and work completed, alongside open questions and responsible roles. The source list shows which statement each source supports. The version record identifies this edition for citation; private household documents stay unpublished.
