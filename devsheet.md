# IMOBX

## Pilot and building guide

**Project proposal · Version 0.8, 24 October 2026**

### 1. Homes with a path to ownership

IMOBX is preparing a rent-to-own housing project in Rio de Janeiro. Under the plan, a family rents a home under an agreement explaining how it becomes the owner. The organization arranges access to property, contracts construction and manages repairs and accounts. The first project is for families who have been displaced or cannot access ordinary housing finance. Location and payments need to fit each household’s circumstances, including work, school, transport and support networks.

**What exists today.** Rafael’s dated village budget and the separate two-loft account; drawings; a village illustration made with an image generator; and construction photographs supplied by the team. The drawings and photographs are not a listing of available homes. Rafael describes his construction experience and local knowledge; his references are not yet listed. John works on communication and digital tools.

**What is not decided.** The property. The home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay, and how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Repairs, missed payments and leaving. IMOBX’s legal form, the terms for using each property, and Rafael’s and John’s responsibilities, which need written agreements. Funding and what funders receive. The rights of any token, which is a digital record of a right that can be bought and sold.

### How the operation fits together

| Part | How it works |
|---|---|
| Homes | Documents showing ownership or the legal right to use the property, a building budget and a clear family agreement. |
| Money | Separate records for funding, payments, costs and amounts set aside. |
| Decisions | Defined powers for residents, staff and any investors. |
| Services | A way to request repairs, follow progress and question decisions. |

Shared designs, purchasing and administration can make further projects easier to deliver. Each family has a route to owning its whole home. Residents help shape shared spaces and local priorities.

This guide explains how a request leads to completed work, a check that it meets the agreement, and payment. The [project reference](reference.html) records evidence, responsibilities and sources in Portuguese and English. The [whitepaper](whitepaper.html) explains the model; the [roadmap](roadmap.html) sets out stages and conditions for proceeding.

## 2. Housing designs and building costs

### Rafael’s village proposal

Rafael’s plan brings together 45 m² two-level homes around a shared courtyard, with solar panels, fruit trees, play space and two study rooms. The [illustration](properties.html#visao-rafael) shows the intended community, rather than a completed development, and it was made with an image generator.

On 30 November 2025, Rafael supplied building calculations using CUB and SINAPI, Brazilian cost references. His revised per-home budget is:

| Item | USD per home |
|---|---:|
| Land | 5,550 |
| House construction, 45 square metres | 18,600 |
| Solar | 1,500 |
| Shared infrastructure and facilities | 1,500 |
| Legal and design | 627 |
| **Total** | **27,777** |

The plan covers at least 36 homes built together over 12 months: **36 × US$27,777 = US$999,972**. These are Rafael’s dated project figures, not a finished-home price published by the indexes. [SINAPI](https://www.caixa.gov.br/poder-publico/modernizacao-gestao/sinapi/paginas/default.aspx) is the Caixa reference for construction costs and inputs, and the [CUB scope page](https://sinduscon-caxias.net.br/servicos/cub) explains what CUB covers; neither gives a price for these homes. A chosen site needs a budget in reais covering its actual work, permissions, services and unexpected costs.

### The separate two-loft case

On 20 January 2026, Rafael reported a contract for two lofts, awaiting its first deposit. **US$27,777 covers both lofts together**, equivalent to US$13,888.50 each by division. The included work, current progress and final costs are not yet documented. This is a separate case from the village budget. See the [drawings and supplied photos](properties.html#estudo-lofts).

## 3. Payments and home ownership

### The family’s agreement

Under the plan, a family rents the home and pays the agreed monthly amount in reais. Its agreement states the purchase price and how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Its purchase record shows how much has counted towards buying the home and how much remains to pay.

For the first household, the home’s price, monthly payment and time to pay have not been set, and neither has how much of each payment counts towards buying the home. Rafael’s construction budgets are evidence for assessing building costs, not household sale offers. The household does not need to buy an investment token or use a crypto wallet. See the [household explanation in Portuguese and English](rent-to-own.html#payments).

Repair charges, missed payments, temporary hardship and leaving need separate terms. If a family leaves, what happens to the money already counted towards buying its home is not agreed. No refund formula or grace period is established here. These terms belong in the offer the household assesses before agreeing to move in.

### What the money pays for

The budget covers taxes, property costs, maintenance, administration, the cost of keeping promises to families and money set aside for unexpected expenses. New construction and any investor payments need their own agreed funding and terms. Donations and grants keep their specified uses.

Each amount received is counted once. An amount credited toward buying the home records the family’s progress; it is not another receipt of cash. The spending plan needs to preserve the organization’s ability to complete the promised home transfer.

### When the home becomes the family’s

Once the purchase conditions are met, the required documents and registration transfer the whole home to the household. A token transfer does not perform that property transfer. IMOBX stops receiving rent from the home. Continued services and further construction need their own funding; any ongoing service charge needs an actual service and terms the household agreed to.

The financial plan needs to show costs and cash through construction, occupancy and transfer. It also needs to account for delays, major repairs and periods when a household cannot pay. See [funding and spending](transparency.html).

## 4. Funding construction and growth

The first project needs funding for its contracted work. Sources under consideration are donations, grants, service income and investment that the chosen organisation is legally allowed to offer. The token proposal aims to fund housing construction and expansion and, on separate budget lines, branding and a community outreach programme. Housing, outreach and any investor payments need their own approved budgets. Token rights remain under study; the [four paths](reference.html#token) range from proceeding without an IMOBX token to an investment in the operation.

### New investment and later trading

Under the token proposal, investors buy tokens directly from IMOBX to help fund its work. What buyers receive in return is not agreed. A later sale between investors normally pays the seller. A separately collected trading fee can provide additional money to the project where the agreement allows it.

The housing budget needs to work through periods of little or no trading income. The accounts show the fees actually received and their agreed use.

### Money available for building

Funds committed to a trading market are unavailable for construction while that commitment lasts. The accounts distinguish money available for bills, amounts reserved for families and repairs, and other commitments.

New investment can pay for agreed work before families start paying rent. First, IMOBX needs its legal form, documents showing the right to use the property, a budget and a legally allowed way to raise that money. Forecast rent is not money already received. Payments to investors depend on the chosen agreement and actual available funds. See the [funding questions](transparency.html#faq-funding).

### How outreach work is paid

An approved campaign budget pays for specific tasks, with the work and payment terms written down first. A participant submits their work; a reviewer checks that it matches the agreed task before approving payment. The community tool can show submission, review and payment status. Paid participants need to disclose compensation and follow each channel’s rules.

Work payments and passive investment income need separate terms. The token model and its rights remain under research. Residents do not need to promote the project to keep housing or decision rights.

## 5. Participation and decision rights

A family’s housing agreement, its membership voice and an investment are separate relationships. One person can hold more than one role.

| Participant | What needs to be clear |
|---|---|
| Families | Affordable payments, repairs, purchase progress, hardship, leaving and whole-home transfer. |
| Property contributors | Donation, lease or sale terms; title, debts and transfer costs. |
| Staff and resident representatives | Powers, pay, appointment, spending limits, reporting and replacement. |
| Builders and suppliers | Work, price, payment stages and independent completion checks. |
| Donors and social funders | Permitted use, reports, delays and cancellation. |
| Possible investors | Who would create the token, what buyers would receive, where income comes from, costs, risk of loss, voting, sales and leaving. |
| Neighbours | Local impacts, services, access and a way to raise concerns. |
| Independent checkers | Authority and access to review property, safety, accounts and agreements. |

Study-room hours, courtyard use, shared-space repair priorities and representative selection are examples of resident decisions. One option under discussion is one vote per member for those matters. Staff have agreed duties and spending limits, with someone else checking completed work. Investor rights follow the selected investment agreement.

A household’s purchase terms follow its agreement. A vote, including a token vote, cannot on its own change a family’s housing agreement, and buying tokens does not give control over another family’s contract. Residents’ say does not depend on investing. Investors receive only the rights written in their agreement. The voting procedure, spending limits and dispute review process are not designed yet; they are developed with participants. See the [decisions and repair examples](rent-to-own.html#decisions).

Elinor Ostrom’s work on shared resources, described in [section 7 of the whitepaper](whitepaper.html#7-resident-voice-and-accountable-governance), informs questions about membership, shared rules, checking work and resolving disputes. These questions apply to facilities such as solar equipment, the orchard and study rooms. See the [decision examples](roadmap.html#governance) and the [participant map](roadmap.html#participation). Brazil’s securities-market regulator, the CVM, looks at the rights a token carries, not its label, in [Guidance Opinion 40](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/legislacao/pareceres-orientacao/pare040.html); the opinion does not approve any IMOBX offer.

## 6. Records, payments and support

Under the plan, digital services help people read agreements, request repairs, follow work and check approved information. Private family documents stay outside the public website and outside any blockchain, which is a shared digital record that shows who approved a payment.

| Record or service | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Project website | Explain the proposal, current progress, costs and documents. |
| Support channel | Receive questions and requests, with a visible response and status. |
| Legal records | Document the legal right to use the property, family agreements and any investment. |
| Accounts | Connect each amount received and paid to its purpose and evidence. |
| Shared work record | Show responsibilities, decisions, deadlines and work checked against the agreement. |

### Following a payment

Families pay in reais. IMOBX keeps money for Brazilian bills. Any approved digital payment needs a provider that accepts the organization and its activities, a record of conversion costs and a tested return to the bank. No provider is confirmed for IMOBX. See the [money path](transparency.html#settlement).

<div id="distribution-accounting-and-the-legacy-euler-approach"></div>

If an investment permits income payments, software can keep a record of what each eligible person can claim. Running costs include setup, individual claims, corrections and support. No operating-cost saving is established yet.

### How a repair moves forward

A resident reports a leak. The maintenance role assesses it and obtains a quote. Spending follows the agreed budget and approval rule. Another person checks the completed repair; the accounts link its payment and receipt. The resident can question the result. Records of unfinished work let a replacement continue when someone leaves a role.

The complete process, including payment failures and lost access, still needs to be demonstrated. A public report shows the approved budget, the work completed, who checked it and the amount paid. It identifies who prepared and reviewed the accounts. Private identity and household documents stay outside the public site. See [everyday decisions and tasks](transparency.html#faq-flock-siah).

## 7. Development stages

IMOBX’s development plan connects the first homes in Rio with resident participation, transparent accounts and additional funding. Legal preparation, community discussions and digital trials can progress in parallel. Construction dates depend on each project, funding and permissions.

| Stage | Intended result |
|---|---|
| Legal structure and property | A responsible organization, documents showing the legal right to use the property and reviewed housing agreements. |
| Resident participation | A say in shared matters without buying investment tokens, with defined budgets and review procedures. |
| First homes | Funded construction, safe occupancy and records of household payments and purchase progress. |
| Payment records people can check | A demonstration of USDC, a digital currency designed to track the US dollar, received and payments approved on Arc, a blockchain network, linked to accounts and evidence. |
| Funding and account review | Records of where funding comes from, which spending is approved and how promises to families are paid for. |
| More homes and owner partnerships | Further projects with local teams and possible rent-to-own services for participating owners. |

### Following an operating project

The monthly report shows receipts and payments, amounts set aside, building costs and delays, repairs, unresolved issues and families’ purchase progress. Every amount received and paid is checked against its records. Affordability and service-response targets need to be agreed locally.

A first expansion review comes after at least three operating months. This is a review point, not proof of long-term viability. A second local team should be able to repeat the process, with funding for new work that respects existing commitments. The detailed roadmap explains who does the work, what must be ready and what stops a stage. Named appointments and delivery dates are open.

### What the public can follow

The website explains the proposal now. As the project develops, reports show signed agreements, approved work, funding received, construction and home transfers. Any investment access follows defined rights, legal review and checked payment arrangements. See the [detailed roadmap](roadmap.html).

## 8. Project status and next steps

The housing operation and financial token remain in preparation. Published drawings and budgets help explain the intended work; they do not establish completed homes or an available investment.

| Area | Current position |
|---|---|
| Design and costs | Rafael’s dated village budget and the separate two-loft account are available. |
| Property | The first project needs documented ownership or authority to use the site. |
| Organization | Legal form, accountable roles and agreements remain to be finalized. |
| Families | Eligibility, affordability, individual terms and shared decision rights need local discussion. |
| Funding | No received grant, investment amount or donated-property count is confirmed here. |
| Token | Housing construction and expansion and, separately, branding and outreach are intended uses. The issuer, financial rights, supply, access, income and transfer terms remain under study. |
| Payments | Provider acceptance and the complete payment route remain to be demonstrated. |

### Evidence for the next project decision

The readiness review brings together the property documents, site-specific budget, construction permissions, family terms, responsibilities and funding plan. Relevant building, accounting and legal professionals review their parts. Participants can see which commitments are agreed and which questions remain open.

### Following progress

Planned reports distinguish proposed, signed, paid and completed work. Residents have access to their own agreements and purchase records. Public accounts explain project spending without exposing household documents.

The [project reference](reference.html#chapters) maps Rafael’s manuscript chapters to the current proposal and sources. It identifies open work, including the complete financial model and professional review. [Photos and drawings](properties.html) show the supplied design and site material with captions; they are not a listing of available homes. No investment is offered through this guide.
