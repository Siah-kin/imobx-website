01 / THE PROJECT

Rent a home. Work toward owning it.

IMOBX is preparing a first rent-to-own housing project in Rio.

  • Local construction and clear family agreements.
  • Residents help shape shared spaces and decisions.
  • Current stage: planning, documents and design.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT01 / 12
Presenter notes

Rafael brings construction and local experience; John handles community and digital work. Their roles, the legal organization and the right to use each property need written agreements. This draft explains the plan and asks for partners; it does not offer an investment.

02 / THE PEOPLE

Start with what a family needs.

The first project would help displaced families and people unable to use ordinary housing finance.

  • Agree who can join and what payments are affordable.
  • Explain repairs, payments and the path to buying the home.
  • Make help and complaints easy to access.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT02 / 12
Presenter notes

Brazil’s 2022 Census counted about 16.4 million residents in favelas and urban communities, 8.1% of the population. That is national context, not a description of a selected family or site. Living in a favela does not itself mean being displaced. Listen to families about work, schools, transport, support networks and whether a move suits them. No pilot families are claimed as enrolled.

04 / THE OPERATING MODEL

Put the home and responsibilities in writing.

IMOBX would arrange property access, family agreements and building work.

  • Check who owns the property and how IMOBX may use it.
  • Agree the builder’s work, price, deadline and who checks it.
  • Explain payments, purchase progress and the final transfer.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT04 / 12
Presenter notes

A promise to donate is different from a completed property transfer. A founder linked to the builder should not approve its own bill. Brazilian advisers need to review the property and legal arrangement. A token does not replace title registration.

05 / MONEY

Show where each payment goes.

Keep rent, donations, grants, service income and trading fees separately identified.

  • Pay taxes, repairs and agreed running costs.
  • Keep money for empty homes and commitments to families.
  • Fund more homes; consider any permitted investor payments afterward.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT05 / 12
Presenter notes

No old percentage split is agreed. Money credited toward a family’s purchase is not a second receipt of cash. Money paid by a buyer to a token seller normally goes to that seller, apart from separately collected fees. Money locked in a trading market is not also available to build homes.

06 / CONTINUITY

Pay today’s bills. Plan the next homes.

Running the existing homes and building more need separate budgets.

  • Check the budget with little or no trading income.
  • Rent ends when the family becomes the home’s owner.
  • Show how continuing income and new building funds will be found.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT06 / 12
Presenter notes

Having a thousand homes does not itself show that the accounts balance. One-off grants can help construction but do not become continuing income. Any ongoing charge needs a real, affordable service under agreed terms. Reviews after three or six years are not promises of payouts.

07 / RESIDENT VOICE

Give residents a real say.

Discuss which decisions belong to residents, workers and possible investors.

  • Residents help decide how shared spaces and repairs work.
  • People chosen for work have time limits and can be replaced.
  • Housing and purchase rights stay protected by agreements and law.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT07 / 12
Presenter notes

One option is equal member voice in resident matters, elected people responsible for work, and investor rights limited to the written investment agreement. Buying tokens would not buy membership or control over every housing decision. Ostrom’s ideas help us ask who participates, who checks and how disputes are resolved. These are proposed rules to agree. Online activity and scores do not decide housing rights.

08 / DIGITAL TOOLS

Make fair cooperation easier.

SIAH Protocol is the brand. Hive DAO is how we aim to share responsibility.

  • See what needs doing, choose work and leave useful records.
  • Software agents help track people, wallets, tasks and approvals.
  • The Flock is the Telegram bot; Arc and Arctide are possible payment tools.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT08 / 12
Presenter notes

Hive DAO is the operating model. The aim is for work to continue without constant founder instructions, with room to disagree and ask for review. For example: report a leak, assess it, approve a repair, check delivery and payment, then leave the record ready for the next person. The plan combines Ostrom’s ideas, agents written in Python, Arc payments and possible Arctide project fees. The complete process still needs to be built and demonstrated in IMOBX. The Flock can serve other projects too; other trading apps need their own integration checks.

09 / TOKEN DECISION

Decide what a token would actually give.

A community membership record and an investment do different jobs.

  • Choose the legal form and keep donations for their agreed purpose.
  • Explain who owes what, where payment money comes from and how to leave.
  • Using money to build more homes does not automatically raise a token’s price.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT09 / 12
Presenter notes

The nonprofit idea keeps resources serving housing. Under Law 13.019’s definition for public-sector partnerships, nonprofits use results for their social purpose. A nonprofit cannot simply promise those results as investor dividends. Brazilian advisers need to review any other permitted investment arrangement. No supply, return rate, sale date or resident token plan is agreed. The token’s name does not decide its legal treatment.

10 / THE ROADMAP

Prepare, test, then decide the next step.

The roadmap links homes, money, shared decisions and digital tools.

  • Days 1–30: roles, property, budget and family discussions.
  • Days 31–60: money rules and worked examples; day 90: readiness decision.
  • Fund the pilot, follow results and test with another local team.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT10 / 12
Presenter notes

These are preparation targets from an agreed start, not promised building dates. Construction needs property, funding and permissions. Follow at least three operating months before the first expansion review. Website v1 explains the plan; v2 reports real building and accounts without needing a token. Any later token access follows agreed rights and checked legal and payment arrangements.

11 / PILOT EVIDENCE

Measure homes and promises kept.

Follow actual costs, accounts and families’ experience.

  • Check completed work, final cost and delays.
  • Match every receipt and payment; report repairs and unresolved issues.
  • Show participation and progress toward owning the home.
IMOBX / PARTNER DISCUSSION DRAFT11 / 12
Presenter notes

Agree response times and affordability targets with local participants. Report homes as planned, contracted, delivered or transferred, with checked records. Token prices and holder counts do not measure how well families are housed. No completed transfers or committed capital are invented here.

  • Building partners: confirm the work, property access, costs and timing.
  • Housing, accounting and legal advisers: review agreements and money rules.
  • Families and community partners: test the information and help process.
